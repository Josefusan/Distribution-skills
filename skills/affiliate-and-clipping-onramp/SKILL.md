# Affiliate and clipping on-ramp

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, attributed to him rather than presented as universal facts.

Traffic is the transferable skill. EP's thesis: for your first $10k online, skip creating a product - find something people already buy and help the sellers sell it. Clipping/UGC is the purest filter (attention in, dollars out, no conversion step), affiliate is where the same skill pays far more, and long-form YouTube affiliate content is where it compounds into passive income. This skill gives the reasoning, an offer-vetting scorecard, and a 90-day on-ramp with milestones.

## When to use

- User is a beginner asking how to make money online, with little capital, skill, or audience.
- User wants to build a product/SaaS/course first and hasn't ever driven converting traffic.
- User asks about clipping, UGC, Whop, affiliate networks, CPA/CPI offers, or which offer to pick.
- User has an affiliate link and doesn't know what to do with it.
- User asks whether YouTube or short-form is better for affiliate income.
- User asks how to approach an offer owner or negotiate a commission.

## Core principles

**Traffic is the transferable skill; learn it before building anything.** EP (2025, reaffirmed 2026): for your first $10k, pick a solid affiliate program and pour all effort into learning to drive traffic and test what converts. Successful affiliate marketers succeed at every subsequent venture; money follows whoever can reliably send converting traffic.

**"Find something people buy, and help the sellers sell it."** The simplest entry: browse affiliate networks for a product proven to convert, learn to make content that influences people to buy it. EP started at 14 sending pay-per-install links to strangers on Snapchat at $1/install for ~$100/day. The entry bar is agency, not capital or skill.

**The simplest path to a few thousand a month:** find a valuable offer, contact the owner, create content for their ICP, capture leads with a lead magnet, send them over for a commission. Protected by two filters: most people never understand the right things, and most who understand fail on implementation.

**Niche and lander matter more than traffic volume - same clicks, 10x revenue.** At 16 EP ran ~20,000 clicks/day to a content locker for ~$1,500/day. A later product converted at ~$2,000 per 1,000 landing-page visits; the same volume would have been ~$20,000/day. Maximize payout per visitor before scaling traffic.

**The first few dollars are psychological, not financial.** Fastest first taste: join a network with cost-per-install offers, send your link to friends. Expect trivial amounts and possibly bans - but seeing money appear "from nothing" is the spark that drives everything after.

**Clipping is the ultimate filter.** It compresses online business to attention in, dollars out, with conversion removed. 100k daily views on TikTok/IG is very achievable, making $100/day realistic. If you can't clear $1k/month with the simplest model, no other model will save you; if you can, you've built the skill every other model needs.

**The payout curve is brutal: ~1% of clippers take ~95% of the payout.** Don't treat clipping as an averages game. The play is top 1% on quality and volume or the expected value is near zero.

**Choose faceless UGC over plain clipping.** Whop rates (2025-03): ~$1/1,000 views for clips, ~$2/1,000 for faceless UGC, ~$3.50/1,000 for regular UGC. Faceless UGC teaches scripting, recording, editing, and what gets views; adding an affiliate link teaches converting views to sign-ups. Don't buy info products at this stage - traffic is learned by doing.

**Pick offers with performance data, not vibes.** Whop's affiliate dashboard lets you search by existing affiliates' earnings, conversion rate, and EPC, and view promo assets. Treat any marketplace like a real network: filter for proven metrics before committing traffic.

**Talk to offer owners.** Picking the right offer is the biggest variable in results; owners know what converts and how to position it.

**Check the incentives behind free "methods".** Someone posts a free method, hundreds run it, the poster earns from everyone sent to the network he's affiliated with. Read every free method as a consumer with this in mind.

**Educate the market for free, supply the product through affiliates.** EP's peptides-niche example: content teaches the category and builds demand; affiliates capture it. Education creates demand.

**Long-form YouTube compounds into truly passive income.** Case study: faceless, outsourced long-form videos promoting a Whop community hit $450-$600/day in commissions within 15 days, all organic, 100% margin. Separately, videos uploaded three months earlier were still netting $7k/month after EP abandoned the project - unlike feed-based short-form.

## Workflow

The 90-day on-ramp. Print the checklist (EP: "get addicted to ticking off checklists").

### Days 1-7: the spark

1. Sign up to an affiliate network with cost-per-install or simple offers. Send your link to friends. Goal: see the first dollars appear. Expect it to be trivial and possibly to get banned - the point is the psychological spark.
2. Sign up to Whop (or the equivalent clipping/UGC marketplace) and pick one faceless-UGC program. Decision point: if the user is camera-comfortable and wants the higher rate, regular UGC; otherwise faceless UGC. Avoid plain clipping unless they only want the pure attention loop.
3. Do not buy any course. Set a hard rule: traffic skills are learned by doing.

### Days 8-30: the filter (clipping/UGC)

4. Post daily. Study what gets views in the niche. Track views per post and $/1,000 views.
5. Milestone at day 30: on pace for $1k/month from the simplest model? If no, this is the diagnostic: keep iterating on the filter before adding complexity. If yes, you have the traffic skill - proceed.
6. Zero-capital variant if stuck for content: film your monitor breaking down interesting Whop communities (how much they make, how they acquire members), Whop affiliate link in bio. EP's bar: $100/day minimum for someone serious.

### Days 31-60: the offer (affiliate)

7. Vet 5-10 offers with the scorecard below. Filter for proven conversion metrics (EPC, conversion rate, existing affiliates' earnings, commission 50%+ where available).
8. Pick one. DM the owner casually, peer-to-peer, not as a vendor (see cold-DM note). Ask what converts and how they position it.
9. Make content targeted at that offer's ICP. Build a lead magnet; capture emails (or Telegram) so unconverted traffic is stored, not lost. Add the affiliate link inside the magnet and the owned channel.
10. Decision point on medium:
    - Sophisticated/high-intent buyers, or you want compounding: long-form faceless YouTube about the problem the product solves, structured like a VSL where the product is the solution.
    - Mass/beginner audience, fast iteration: short-form educational content feeding the list.
    - Skip X until experienced - EP calls it a far more sophisticated market.
11. Milestone at day 60: first affiliate commission. Then check payout per visitor before touching volume.

### Days 61-90: compound

12. Apply the "niche and lander > volume" rule: if revenue per 1,000 visits is low, change the offer or the lander before scaling. Same clicks can be 10x revenue.
13. Graduate into paid short-form partnerships: info-product sellers, software, mobile apps, ecommerce brands selling through short-form. Take affiliate deals.
14. Keep publishing long-form. Goal: videos that keep paying after you stop.
15. Milestone at day 90: a repeatable loop (content -> list -> affiliate link), one long-form channel, and a shortlist of owners you have relationships with. Only now consider whether to build your own product - and if so, your list is the launch asset.

## Checklists / templates

### Offer-vetting scorecard

Score 0-2 each. Promote only offers scoring 10+ of 16; anything with a 0 on rows 1-3 is out.

| # | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Proof it converts (EPC / conversion rate visible on the dashboard) | no data | some data | strong EPC + conversion rate |
| 2 | Existing affiliates are earning | none visible | a few | multiple affiliates with real earnings |
| 3 | Commission | under 30% | 30-49% | 50%+ or high fixed payout |
| 4 | Payout per visitor (revenue per 1,000 landing visits) | unknown/low | moderate | high - the offer, not the traffic, does the work |
| 5 | Niche is high-value (trading, crypto, bizopp, sports betting, software) | low-ticket | mid | high-value |
| 6 | Owner is reachable and shares what converts | no | responsive | actively helps with positioning |
| 7 | Promo assets / lander quality | none | usable | proven lander + assets |
| 8 | Incentive check: is the "opportunity" itself an affiliate funnel for someone else? | yes, clearly | unclear | offer is the product, not a network referral |

### Owner outreach (cold DM) note

EP (2025-09): "yo" then "got an opportunity for you, think we can cook" gets entertained; "let's say we came into your business and added 100 signups a month" gets ignored. The 90% of failed cold DMs translate to "I can see you're making money, let me have some of it." Approach as a collaborator, not a vendor. Ask what converts.

### 90-day milestone checklist (print it)

- [ ] Day 1-7: first dollars seen from an affiliate/CPI link
- [ ] Day 7: one faceless-UGC program joined, posting daily
- [ ] Day 30: on pace for $1k/month from clipping/UGC (or diagnosed why not)
- [ ] Day 45: 5-10 offers scored; one chosen; owner contacted
- [ ] Day 50: lead magnet live; owned channel (email/Telegram) capturing traffic
- [ ] Day 60: first affiliate commission
- [ ] Day 75: revenue per 1,000 visits measured; offer/lander adjusted before scaling
- [ ] Day 90: one long-form channel publishing; one paid partnership conversation open

### Research note: where to learn the technical side

EP (2025-02): on YouTube, production value inversely correlates with information density. The best technical info comes from tiny channels presenting slideshows; videos above ~50k views are optimized for entertainment. Deliberately seek low-subscriber, low-production expert content.

## Anti-patterns

- Building a product, SaaS, or course before you've ever sent converting traffic.
- Buying info products during the clipping stage instead of learning by doing.
- Treating clipping as an averages game. 1% take 95%.
- Scaling traffic to a weak offer. EP's 20,000 clicks/day into $1 installs is his named "big mistake" - the same clicks on a better offer were 10x.
- Picking offers by vibes or by a guru's recommendation without checking whose affiliate link you're being funneled through.
- Sending traffic straight to the affiliate link with no owned channel. Unconverted traffic is lost.
- Vendor-style polished cold DMs to offer owners.
- Starting on X as a beginner. Sophisticated market; learn elsewhere first.
- Learning from high-production, high-view "educational" YouTube - it's optimized for entertainment.
