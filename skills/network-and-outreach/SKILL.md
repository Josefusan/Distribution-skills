# Network and Outreach

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. Opinionated operator heuristics, attributed to EP throughout.

Core thesis: per EP, 90% of success is network, and the formula is mechanical: become great at something that benefits powerful people's business or life, be useful to them, and the network forms. Nobody helps someone who offers no value. The cheapest education is working inside a successful operation, the right teachers are one or two levels above you, and a single well-targeted cold DM can compound absurdly (one forum DM led EP to a partnership worth $250k in a year). This skill turns that into a step-by-step path from zero contacts to group chats, with DM templates in EP's own phrasing.

## When to use

- User has no contacts in their target industry and asks how to start.
- User asks whether to pay for a mentorship, mastermind, or one-on-one call.
- User wants to learn from a famous billionaire/founder's advice.
- User is drafting outreach to an operator, agency, or potential partner.
- User asks how to get a first job or role in online business with no credentials.
- User asks whether to cut off old friends to "level up".
- User wants to help friends get into online business and is frustrated they don't act.
- User asks where the "real" conversations about a niche happen.

## Core principles

**Learn from people one or two levels above you, not billionaires.** Someone making $10k-$50k/month online is far better positioned to teach a beginner how to reach $10k/month than Bezos or Musk. People at the top for years are foreign to the ground level, which is why their advice reduces to "work hard, focus on changing the world." People slightly ahead remember the terrain, their tactics apply at your scale, and their help costs less. Source that tier proactively.

**Work inside a successful operation; it is the cheapest education available.** EP credits much of what he knows to running a short-form marketing agency, a behind-the-scenes view that cannot be transmitted in words. Nine out of ten people would get further helping someone established than insisting on being the face of their own brand from day one; ego is a common cause of failure.

**The mentorship price filter.** Per EP, nobody worthwhile mentors one-on-one for under about $5k; a $250 one-on-one call from someone claiming wealth is a red flag because the opportunity-cost math doesn't work for a real operator. Cheap productized info (documents, communities) from legit operators is fine because it scales; cheap personal time is the tell.

**Give value first, and give without expecting return.** Networking is never one-sided extraction. If you want help, figure out how to help them first. The network that drives most of EP's income came from freely giving help and insights.

**Proximity is the highest-ROI activity.** It is much easier to succeed when you regularly talk to people no smarter than you who are hitting your goals; proximity normalizes the goal and transfers tactics. One friend who has already made it is real-life proof; communities are the substitute if you lack that friend.

**Get into group chats and niche communities.** The next big companies are being plotted in random Telegram groups (EP's version of Napoleon Hill's mastermind). People who succeed online are invariably active in niche communities: forums like BlackHatWorld, or simply being active on X. Daily participation supplies information intake, idea iteration, and immediate feedback you can't get alone.

**Run two social circles.** Keep the friends you grew up with unless they are an actively bad influence; cutting them off is over-glorified hustle advice. Separately build a circle of business peers who raise your baseline: if your circle makes $100k/month, $25k/month feels easy. To enter valuable circles you must first become valuable. Nobody on money-Twitter is your friend by default; don't confuse networking with friendship, though real friendships can grow from shared incentives.

**Cold outreach compounds; competency is the entire filter.** Profitable operators have piles of tasks to delegate but can't trust the 95% of applicants who show none. Portfolio, previous work, and a precise offer. With sophisticated buyers skip scripted persuasion: EP instantly blocks "let's hop on a call to discuss pricing" pitches. State the service and price upfront; nobody reads long AI-written paragraphs.

**Publicly posted expertise is a living resume.** While "autistically posting AI sauce," EP received numerous job offers in his DMs as a 16-year-old dropout with no technical background. Demonstrated knowledge in public outperforms credentials.

**Qualify the friends you try to help.** Despite five-figure-day screenshots and repeated explanations, only one of EP's real-life friends had the discipline to build an income stream. Still try to put people on, but learn to identify who actually wants the assistance.

**Formal education lags; build a self-education loop.** Curricula run roughly a decade behind (a fourth-year data analytics student who had never heard of Cursor or the term IDE). Use AI as the tutor, and re-read formative books after you have lived the concepts; time applying beats time consuming roughly 100 to 1.

## Workflow

Build a network from zero. Run the steps in order; each step earns the next.

1. **Pick the one skill you will be known for.** Something popular and in demand that benefits operators' business (EP's example: popular video editing styles; also copywriting, design, media buying, outreach, automation). Learn it by building samples immediately. Without a demonstrable skill there is no step 2.
   - If the user has no skill yet: this is the whole task for the next few weeks. See `motivation-identity-and-career-strategy` for the skill-foundation checklist.

2. **Build a small portfolio of proof.** Three to five finished pieces in the style the target operators actually use. This is the filter operators apply; skipping it puts you in the 95% who show no competency.

3. **Pick 2-3 communities where your target tier hangs out.** Niche forums, X (follow and reply to operators one or two levels above you), Telegram/Discord groups of a specific model. Apply the "who to learn from" filter below to choose whom to follow. Ignore billionaire content.

4. **Give value publicly for 2-4 weeks before asking for anything.** Post what you learn as you build ("learned something interesting → post about it"). Reply to operators with useful, specific observations. Answer questions in the community. This is the living resume and it is how DMs start coming to you.

5. **Send targeted cold DMs using the formats below.** Two kinds:
   - *Work-for-free offer* to a successful operation (EP's 5-step roadmap: learn the skill → ask to work for free → ask for compensation once integral → observe how the operation runs behind the scenes → build something similar of your own).
   - *Precise paid offer* to a buyer: service + price upfront, portfolio attached, no call request.
   Never send long paragraphs. Never open with "let's hop on a call."

6. **Become integral, then ask for compensation.** Once inside, do the boring, delegated work extremely well. Study how the operation works behind the scenes (that is the real payment). When you are integral, ask for pay.

7. **Move into group chats.** Ask the operators you have helped which group chats they are in and whether you can be added; contribute daily (ideas, findings, feedback). This is where the unfiltered idea exchange happens.

8. **Maintain the two circles.** Keep old friends. Keep the business circle explicitly on mutual benefit; keep giving first. Periodically re-check that the business circle's baseline is above yours.

9. **When friends ask you to put them on, qualify before investing time.** Give the full model once; see who acts within a week; spend your time on those who do.

## Checklists / templates

### "Who to learn from" filter

Follow, DM, or pay for someone only if most of these are true:

- [ ] They are one or two levels above you (e.g. $10k-$50k/month when you are at $0-$10k), not a billionaire or a decade-removed founder.
- [ ] Their tactics still apply at your scale, and they can describe the ground-level terrain in specifics, not platitudes.
- [ ] They are an active operator in a niche community, not only a broadcaster.
- [ ] If they sell time one-on-one, the price passes EP's filter (worthwhile mentors don't sell one-on-one time for under about $5k; a $250 "call" is a red flag). Cheap documents or communities are fine.
- [ ] They put family and real work above flexing (see `money-management-and-advice-filtering` for the full grifter filter).

### Cold DM templates (EP's phrasing)

Precise paid offer (the format EP prefers to receive):

```
yo, I offer X, let's work
```

Expanded, still short:

```
yo — I do <service> for <type of operator>. <One line of proof: link to portfolio /
"did this for <account>"/ result>. $<price> per <unit>. If useful, reply and I'll
start on <specific first deliverable> this week.
```

Work-for-free entry into a successful operation:

```
Hey — I've been studying how you run <operation>. I can <skill> in the style you use
(<link to 2-3 samples>). I want to learn how you run this behind the scenes, so I'll do
<specific task> for free for <period>. If it's useful, we talk comp after. Want it?
```

Rules for every DM:
- State the service and, for paid offers, the price upfront.
- Attach or link the portfolio; competency is the filter.
- No "let's hop on a call to discuss pricing." No long AI-written paragraphs.
- One ask, one next step.

### Community participation checklist (daily)

- [ ] One useful reply or post in each chosen community.
- [ ] One thing learned today posted publicly.
- [ ] One targeted DM sent (offer, help, or a specific question) to someone one or two levels above.
- [ ] Anything you were helped with: pay it forward once.

### How an operator vets inbound (from EP's partner application)

EP's own partner-program application, reproduced in `references/source-lessons.md`, shows what he filters on when someone reaches out to work with him: what you are genuinely great at and what you have achieved with it, whether income comes from your own business, what you sell, links to your work, and a minimum output commitment. Mirror that in your outreach: lead with specialized knowledge, proof, and a clear commitment.

## Anti-patterns

- Learning from billionaires and celebrity founders; their advice reduces to platitudes for your stage.
- Paying $250 for a one-on-one call with someone "rich"; per EP the price itself is the tell.
- Asking for help before offering any; one-sided extraction.
- "Let's hop on a call to discuss pricing" and long scripted persuasion to sophisticated buyers. EP blocks these instantly.
- Insisting on being the face of your own brand from day one instead of helping someone established (ego).
- Cutting off childhood friends as a hustle ritual.
- Treating money-Twitter acquaintances as friends by default.
- "Business talk" in group chats as a substitute for doing the work (see `execution-and-focus-systems`).
- Repeatedly re-explaining the model to friends who never act.
