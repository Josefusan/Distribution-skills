# Distribution-First Strategy

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill helps you choose, validate, and own a distribution channel for a given product. Core thesis: all roads lead to distribution. A 10x worse product with the right distribution beats the best product with none, so the sequence is: understand the offer, find where the buyers already look, position there, funnel to an owned channel, and only then invest in building.

## When to use

- The user has (or is about to build) a product and has no concrete answer to "where will the first 100 buyers come from?"
- The user says "build it and they will come", "I'll post on Twitter", or "I'll figure out marketing later".
- A product exists but earns nothing; the user assumes the product is the problem.
- A beginner asks what skill to learn first to make money online.
- The user is comparing channels (SEO, Reddit, YouTube, X, ads, communities) for a niche.
- The user wants to sell in content and is hurting engagement with hard sells.

## Core principles

**Distribution beats product.** EP: a 10x worse product with the right distribution outperforms the best product on the market; 99% of vibe-coded software serves an invisible market and earns zero. Treat building as the easy part.

**Traffic generation and direct-response marketing are the two master skills.** EP claims that if you understand both you can never be poor: there are always offers to push and channels to push them in. "Sauce" (secret tactics) is worthless without these fundamentals.

**Marketing is the ultimate portable skill.** Most technical communities lack business and marketing fundamentals, so a marketer can walk into any industry and outcompete more skilled builders. Smarter, more technical people routinely earn less because they cannot position or market.

**Learn distribution before you learn to build, ideally via affiliate marketing for software that already sells.** EP's affiliate curriculum: what niche communities value, what content converts, how to get views, influencing through content, email list building, audience building, selling to cold traffic, paid ads, funnels, copywriting, social algorithms, Google/YouTube SEO. Do not attempt a solo SaaS before this.

**Beginners should not build products at all.** If you have never sold anything, resell a product you already love for a commission. Solving product AND distribution as a first-timer is two unsolved problems at once; tackle one.

**Channel selection is a research task, not a guess (Marketing 101).** Find a product already converting, name the exact problem it solves, list every channel where people with that problem look for solutions, rank them, position there. Shortcut: replicate what already works for competitors.

**Acquire distribution assets instead of building traffic from scratch.** EP's example: an operator bought the top IQ-related subreddits ranking for "IQ test" and pinned his paid test inside. Generalizable: own the communities, forums, and pages that already rank for high-intent keywords in your niche.

**Offer literacy is the real divider.** Knowing what a good offer looks like, whose pain it solves, which corner of the internet holds those people, and the lead-magnet/funnel mechanics. Beginners throw things at the wall; with offer literacy it is hard to fail.

**Never hard-sell in content.** It hurts engagement. Make content maximally educational around the exact problem, funnel viewers to an owned channel (email list or Telegram) with a lead magnet, and sell aggressively there. The owned channel is the breadwinner.

**95% of making money with AI is not AI.** (EP, 2025-12.) It is business fundamentals; AI is a speed multiplier on a sound foundation. Build offer, market understanding, and distribution first.

## Workflow

Walk the user through this when the skill triggers. Do not skip to tactics before step 1 is answered.

1. **State the offer in one sentence.** "[Product] solves [exact problem] for [specific person] resulting in [outcome]." If the user cannot fill this in, stop and route to `offer-design-and-buyer-psychology` first. Distribution cannot be chosen for a vague offer.

2. **Find the proof-of-market product.** Identify at least one competitor or adjacent product that is already converting for this problem. If none exists, flag it: EP requires solid proof a problem exists. If the user is a beginner with nothing sold yet, recommend affiliating for that proven product instead of building.

3. **Map where sufferers look for solutions.** For the exact problem, list candidate touchpoints: Google/YouTube search queries, subreddits, Discord/Telegram/Whop communities, forums, X/TikTok/Instagram/LinkedIn creators the ICP follows, marketplaces and directories. Ask: where would someone with this pain type a query or ask a question?

4. **Reverse-engineer competitors' distribution.** For each proof-of-market product: where does their traffic come from, what content do they post, which creators promote them, which keywords rank? Copy the structure of what works, not the copy itself.

5. **Rank channels with the rubric below.** Score intent, reachability, ownability, and skill fit. Pick ONE primary channel. If the user has capital, check whether a distribution asset (an existing community, page, or ranking site in the niche) can be acquired or partnered with rather than built.

6. **Design the funnel.** Educational content on the primary channel → lead magnet → owned channel (email list or Telegram) → selling happens only in the owned channel. Instrument it from day one so you know which content and source produced each lead.

7. **Validate with real traffic before building more.** Put a purchasable offer (or affiliate link) in front of the channel for a defined period. Decision point: sales come → double down and only now expand product; no sales → diagnose whether the offer or the channel failed (see `offer-design-and-buyer-psychology`), change one variable, retest. If the user has not literally done this loop, they have not tried.

8. **Own the channel.** Once a channel works, convert it into an asset: grow the list, run the community, hold the ranking page. Never let the business depend on one rented platform account.

## Checklists / templates

### Channel ranking rubric (score each channel 1-5)

| Criterion | Question | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Are people here actively seeking a solution to the exact problem (search, "how do I" posts)? | x3 |
| Proof | Is a competitor already converting from this channel? | x3 |
| Ownability | Can traffic be moved to an owned list/community, or can the asset itself be owned? | x2 |
| Skill fit | Does the user already have the skill for this channel (writing, video, SEO, ads)? | x2 |
| Cost to test | Can a real test run within 2-4 weeks and a small budget? | x1 |

Highest weighted score = primary channel. Run only one primary channel until it produces sales.

### Offer-literacy check (answer all before choosing a channel)

- What does a good offer in this niche look like (name one)?
- Whose pain does it solve, specifically?
- Which corner of the internet holds those people?
- What lead magnet pulls them onto an owned channel?
- What does the funnel look like from first touch to purchase?

### Content-to-owned-channel funnel template

1. Educational post/video on the exact problem (no selling).
2. CTA: free lead magnet (template, checklist, tool, mini-course) that solves a slice of the problem.
3. Delivery via email list or Telegram.
4. Sequence in the owned channel: value → proof → offer → hard sell.

### "Nothing is selling" diagnostic

- [ ] Is there a purchasable offer with a buy button live right now? If no, that is the problem.
- [ ] Has real traffic from the chosen channel reached it (not just posts with no clicks)?
- [ ] Is the content educational about the exact problem, or is it selling?
- [ ] Is there an owned channel capturing leads, and is selling happening there?
- [ ] Is a competitor converting from this channel? If not, the channel may be wrong, not the product.

### Beginner path (nothing sold yet)

1. Pick a product you already found amazing that has an affiliate program.
2. Learn its buyers' problem and where they look.
3. Drive traffic and earn commissions.
4. Only after commissions come in, consider your own product.

## Anti-patterns

- Building first and planning marketing later. EP: "build it and they will come" is a route to being broke.
- Attempting a solo SaaS with no prior selling experience.
- Hard-selling inside content; it kills engagement and never builds the owned channel.
- Chasing "sauce" or secret tactics without traffic and direct-response fundamentals.
- Treating AI as the core of the business rather than a multiplier on fundamentals.
- Running many channels at once instead of validating one.
- The IQ-test example includes a free test whose fee appears only at the results screen (a sunk-cost mechanic). Take the asset-acquisition lesson; do not replicate hidden-fee flows.
