# Money Management and Advice Filtering

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. Opinionated operator heuristics, attributed to EP throughout.

**This is not financial, legal, or medical advice.** Claude is not a licensed advisor. This skill gives a procedure for filtering advice and offers and a sanity checklist for volatile income; for actual investment, tax, or health decisions, direct the user to a qualified professional.

Core thesis: internet income is volatile and the game changes fast, so the first real money should be converted into security rather than status; and almost every narrative you meet online is engineered by someone's incentives, so evaluate the incentive, not the claim. EP blew his first few hundred thousand dollars and says being broke in a nice car is more miserable than being broke without one; he also cites a trend that most young online earners are broke within 7 years. The Belle Gibson case is the ceiling on what "advice from a personality" can cost.

## When to use

- User asks whether a specific guru, course, mastermind, mentorship, or "opportunity" is legitimate.
- User quotes an influencer's claim and asks if it's true or asks why they say it.
- User has just made their first meaningful money online and asks what to do with it.
- User wants to make a large lifestyle purchase on new, unproven income.
- User asks about "passive income" claims, theirs or someone else's.
- User is considering a branded framework (named diet, renamed biz-opp, cult-leader system).
- User is about to change a health, legal, or family decision because of an internet personality.
- User sends a pitch heavy on revenue screenshots, supercars, or "I know exactly how this works."

## Core principles

**Ask about incentives, not claims.** Instead of "why does this influencer say X is bad?", ask "what incentive does this influencer have in me believing X is bad?" Per EP this single reframe protects you from most engineered narratives online.

**The one-rule guru filter.** Never take advice from anyone who puts materials or status above taking care of his own family. Supercars-and-girls marketing signals someone optimizing to impress strangers, not a model worth following. EP says this eliminates 90% of gurus.

**Flexing is an inverse signal.** Judge legitimacy by how often someone posts revenue screenshots (most circulating ones are old). If someone needs to flex something, assume it's not part of their everyday life. A $200k car in this space is a marketing expense, not evidence of wealth; some people empty their bank accounts to buy one because it drives impressions. Genuinely successful operators print money quietly.

**Distrust certainty theater.** Nine times out of ten a "know-it-all" persona is a snake-oil setup for selling you something later. The smartest and most honest operators openly admit how much they don't know; intellectual humility is a trust signal.

**Distrust branded frameworks with a single cult-leader figure.** Named diets, renamed biz-opps. Adopting a framework "made for the masses" because the guy "feels legit" outsources your judgment. Take in inputs, run your own experiments, track your own data.

**No advice is a universal law.** Money Twitter generalizes from single experiences: "it failed for us so it never works," "it worked for us so nothing else does." Most contradictory guru claims are both true for their circumstances and false as rules. Apply this to everything, including EP's own heuristics and growth tactics.

**Never take life-affecting advice from internet personalities.** Belle Gibson, an Australian wellness influencer, claimed she cured terminal brain cancer with natural remedies, built an app, cookbook, and large following, collected over $300,000 in charity donations she mostly kept, and never had cancer; followers died after abandoning chemotherapy on her advice. It doubles as a marketing lesson in how powerful, and dangerous, transformation narratives are.

**Don't blow the first real money on status purchases.** Bank the surplus until income is stable and diversified before any lifestyle inflation. Internet income is volatile; you can lose it all within a month.

**Convert internet money into security.** EP's own split: 50% enjoying life, 50% into property and investments. If you're young and printing online, buy assets before lifestyle inflation eats it. Don't be the guy with a supercar and no assets. (This is EP's personal allocation, not a recommendation from Claude.)

**The runway question is the real metric of financial security.** If you stopped working today, how long before you run out of money? EP's own answer was 5+ years; he notes $100k liquid provides life-changing security in a low-cost country. Use the question to test anyone's "passive income" claims, including your own.

## Workflow

### A. "Should I trust this advice / offer?" evaluation

1. **Name the source and the ask.** Who is saying it, what do they want you to believe, and what action does believing it lead to (buy, join, switch, stop treatment, move money)?

2. **Run the incentive question.** "What incentive does this person have in me believing this?" Write the answer down. If the belief leads to a purchase from them or a competitor's loss, weight the claim accordingly.

3. **Apply the one-rule filter.** Does the person visibly put materials or status above their family and real work? If yes, stop; do not take the advice regardless of content.

4. **Check for inverse signals.** Count: revenue screenshots, supercars, lifestyle flexing, "I know exactly how this works" certainty, a single named framework with a leader figure. Each one lowers trust. Admissions of uncertainty and quiet operation raise it.

5. **Check the price-of-time tell (for mentorships/calls).** Cheap productized info from a legit operator is fine; cheap one-on-one time from someone claiming wealth is a red flag (see `network-and-outreach`, mentorship price filter).

6. **Classify the stakes.**
   - *Business tactic, cheap to test:* treat as a hypothesis. Run a small test with your own data within the one-month window (see `execution-and-focus-systems`). Never adopt it as a universal law.
   - *Money-affecting (investment, large purchase, "passive income" scheme):* apply the runway question to their claim and to your own situation; consult a licensed professional before moving money. Claude does not give personalized investment advice.
   - *Life-affecting (health, legal, family):* do not act on the personality's advice. Belle Gibson rule. Route to qualified professionals.

7. **Decide and record why.** Write one line: "Trusting / testing / rejecting because <incentive, signals, stakes>." Revisit it if the person later asks you to buy something.

### B. Personal-finance sanity check for volatile internet income

Not financial advice; a sanity procedure before lifestyle decisions.

1. **Compute the runway.** Liquid savings ÷ monthly essential spend = months of runway if income stopped today. Say the number out loud.
2. **Rate income stability.** How many months has it held? How many sources? One platform, one product, one client = fragile. EP's warning: it can vanish within a month.
3. **Gate lifestyle inflation on stability, not on a good month.** If income is under a few months old or from one source, per EP the surplus gets banked; status purchases wait.
4. **Split deliberately, in writing.** EP's personal rule is 50% life / 50% property and investments. The user picks their own split with a professional; the point is that the split exists before the money arrives, not after.
5. **Test "passive income" claims with the runway question**, including the user's own. Income that requires daily work is not passive.
6. **Re-run quarterly.** Internet income changes fast; the numbers from six months ago are not the numbers now.

## Checklists / templates

### Grifter filter scorecard

Score each 0 (absent) or 1 (present). Higher = less trustworthy.

- [ ] Visible incentive for me to believe this (sells the solution, or a rival's failure benefits them)
- [ ] Materials/status visibly placed above family and real work
- [ ] Frequent revenue screenshots (especially old or unverifiable)
- [ ] Supercar / lifestyle flexing as the main proof
- [ ] "Know-it-all" certainty with no admitted unknowns
- [ ] Branded, named framework with a single leader figure
- [ ] Generalizes one experience into a universal law
- [ ] Cheap one-on-one time offered by someone claiming wealth
- [ ] Transformation narrative that is unverifiable (health, "cured," "went from 0 to 8 figures")

0-1: evaluate on merits and test. 2-3: treat as marketing; test only cheap, reversible pieces. 4+: reject; do not buy, do not act.

### Offer evaluation one-pager

```
SOURCE: <who>            ASK: <what they want me to believe / do>
THEIR INCENTIVE: <what they gain if I believe it>
ONE-RULE FILTER (family > status?): <pass / fail>
INVERSE SIGNALS PRESENT: <list>          TRUST SIGNALS: <admits unknowns? quiet operator?>
STAKES: <business-cheap-to-test / money-affecting / life-affecting>
COST TO TEST SMALL: <$ / hours>          REVERSIBLE?: <y/n>
DECISION: trust / test / reject — because <one line>
PROFESSIONAL REQUIRED?: <yes for money- and life-affecting decisions>
```

### Runway card

```
Liquid savings: <$>      Monthly essentials: <$>      Runway: <months>
Income age: <months>     Sources: <n>                 Stability: fragile / moderate / stable
Planned split (decided with a professional): <life %> / <assets %>
Lifestyle purchase pending: <item, $> → allowed only if stability = stable and runway ≥ <user's threshold>
```

## Anti-patterns

- Asking "is this true?" instead of "who benefits if I believe it?"
- Treating a supercar, watch, or screenshot as proof of a working model.
- Buying because a guru "feels legit" or sounds certain.
- Adopting a named framework wholesale instead of testing pieces with your own data.
- Turning any single tactic (including EP's) into a universal law.
- Changing a medical, legal, or family decision on an influencer's say-so.
- Blowing the first few hundred thousand on status purchases (EP did; he calls it the more miserable version of broke).
- Calling income "passive" that dies the month you stop working.
- Letting lifestyle inflation follow one good month.
- Claude giving personalized investment advice; it is not a licensed advisor and must say so.

## Dated / volatile notes

- "$100k liquid provides life-changing security in a low-cost country" is EP's cost-of-living read as of 2025-2026, not a threshold that survives inflation or location changes.
- "Most young online earners will be broke within 7 years" is EP's stated trend, not a cited study.
- Revenue-screenshot and supercar-flex culture describes money-Twitter/X as of 2026-09-15; the inverse-signal logic applies to whatever the flex du jour becomes.
- The Belle Gibson case is historical (Australia, 2010s) and stable; the lesson does not expire.
