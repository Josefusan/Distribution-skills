# Partnerships, Hiring, and Delegation

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill covers structuring partnerships, hiring and paying people, and designing the business so the owner can step out of daily work. Core thesis: $0 to $10k/month can be done alone; $10k to $100k requires delegation, outsourcing, and automation. Pair around complementary strengths (marketer x developer), pay on performance, lock incentives before delivering value, and restrict your own time to the four productive activities.

## When to use

- Revenue plateau around $10k/month with the owner doing everything.
- Choosing a cofounder or evaluating a partnership offer.
- Hiring developers, designers, editors, creators, or VAs, and deciding what to pay.
- Structuring equity, revshare, affiliate cuts, or referral deals.
- Acquiring clients for a service or agency; cold outreach that isn't working.
- The owner wants passive-for-them income or to build a team.
- Someone asks the user for free help "as a favor".

## Core principles

**The marketer x developer duo is the most profitable two-person partnership online.** Each covers the other's blind spot. If you can't build, sell someone else's software to prove you can distribute, then partner 50/50 with a hungry developer who lacks distribution. Distribution proof is the asset you bring.

**Restructure around strengths instead of fighting weaknesses.** Hate camera? Faceless brand or hire creators. Hate coding? Partner with a dev who hates distribution. Hate sales calls? Partner with a closer. "Discipline" is often a lie told by people who outsource everything they dislike.

**Be the visionary between businessman and technical.** Understand markets, teams, systems, and distribution; be just technical enough to give developers exact logic. Going deeper steals capacity from visionary work when thousands of qualified developers can be hired.

**Hire talent without a personal brand.** Branded providers charge around 4x market worth; a $5,000+ landing page is almost never justified when a skilled unknown builds better for under $500. Source from forums, Discord, and Telegram rather than X. EP cites quotes differing 10x+ for identical builds ($100k vs $8k) and ~$1.7k/month buying very good talent in India or the Philippines.

**When you find a good developer, never let him go and pay him well.** Genuinely good developers are far rarer than assumed; retention is cheaper than replacement.

**Pay on performance, not retainers.** Retainers breed complacency. If someone refuses performance pay, they don't believe in your product or their results, both disqualifying. EP's editor deal: editors keep 100% of ad revenue plus view-milestone bonuses; EP keeps product sales.

**Lock incentives before helping.** 9 out of 10 people you "put on" for free later claim they didn't need your help. Structure equity, revshare, or affiliate cut before delivering value.

**Brokering is underrated.** Millions can be made purely connecting people who want something with people who can fulfil it. EP's AI-adjacent audience generated 10+ service inquiries daily; his plan was to pass leads to a real engineer for referral revenue.

**Teaching publicly beats cold outreach for agency clients.** Cold outreach from an unknown fails on zero credibility. The two working paths: in-person/local connections where credibility transfers, or inbound via publishing the niche-specific work you do.

**Laziness plus ambition is the CEO combination.** Find the path of least resistance and build systems others fulfil. Hard workers hit a ceiling believing they can do everything personally.

**Build a business you can remove yourself from.** Grind at the start: 16-hour days are for testing, validating, and installing systems, never for repeating manual work. EP's former agency runs on his team at ~10 minutes of his time per week, netting ~$8k/month.

**Only four activities are productive.** Improving the product, working on the offer/funnel, creating content/creatives, talking to clients. Learning is "beneficial, not productive" until it feeds one of the four. Twin filter: does this create value for customers or communicate value to prospects?

**Commit to the validated model.** Give a new model 1-2 months of proper effort, then change approach or quit. But model-hopping is among the worst habits; all the models work, the variable is commitment.

## Workflow

### A. Deciding what to delegate

1. **Log a week of the owner's time.** Tag every block as one of: product, offer/funnel, content/creatives, talking to clients, or "other".
2. **Everything in "other" is a delegation or automation candidate.** Decision point: automatable with code → automate; requires judgment or human touch → VA or specialist; owner-only (vision, key relationships) → keep.
3. **Check the profitability trigger.** If the product is already profitable and the owner is avoiding hiring to protect margins, that is a growth killer; hire now.
4. **Systemize before delegating.** Write the process (SOP, Loom) while the owner is still doing it. Delegate only functions the owner has personally validated.
5. **Structure the team.** One trusted personal assistant overseeing everything; each VA assigned to one small compartment so no single person sees enough to replicate the operation.
6. **Kill meetings.** Default to async text and Loom; nearly every "quick call" fits in one paragraph.
7. **Target the operating model.** 100% hands-on during setup (development + distribution), then systems and near-autopilot. Diversify: EP targets at least a quarter of revenue from automated, low-barrier models.

### B. Hiring a specific role

1. **Define the output standard, not the method.** For creatives, never ask whether they use AI; judge only whether the end product meets the bar.
2. **Source off-brand.** Forums, Discord, Telegram, regional freelancer communities. For content talent, gaming content creators: passionate, cheap, DM directly.
3. **Filter.** Trust the first impression: a candidate who can't follow simple application rules will deviate on the job. Ask "Are you the best at what you do?" Run a small paid test task with a simple system to test trainability; expect most to fail.
4. **Pay on performance.** Offer a base tied to output plus milestone bonuses or revenue share. Refusal of performance pay is disqualifying.
5. **Retain the rare good ones.** Pay a proven developer well and keep them.

### C. Structuring a partnership

1. **Confirm complementary blind spots.** Marketer x developer, visionary x builder, operator x closer. Two people with the same skill is not a partnership.
2. **Prove your side first.** If you bring distribution, show it (a channel, a list, affiliate sales). If you bring build, show shipped work.
3. **Lock incentives in writing before any work.** Equity split, revshare, affiliate cut, who owns what, exit terms.
4. **Prefer percentage-of-upside deals over fees** where the user is the leverage: run someone's X presence for a percentage, broker leads for a referral cut, manage creators for a share.
5. **Avoid public beefs** with competitors or ex-partners; feuds hurt both sides.

### D. Getting clients for a service

1. Start with local or in-person connections where credibility transfers.
2. Publish the niche-specific work you do (teach publicly on X); let inbound come.
3. When you do cold outreach: context on why this person specifically, their benefit first, minimal sell.
4. Network counter-cyclically: befriend the sector's influencers when attention has left it.

## Checklists / templates

### Delegation triage

| Task | Productive category (product / offer / content / clients / other) | Automate (code) | Delegate (VA/specialist) | Keep |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rule: everything automatable with code gets code; everything else gets a VA; only the four productive activities stay with the owner, and even those get delegated once systemized.

### Hiring filter

- [ ] Followed the application instructions exactly.
- [ ] Answered "Are you the best at what you do?" with self-awareness.
- [ ] Sourced outside personal-brand marketplaces (no status premium).
- [ ] Passed a small trainability test.
- [ ] Accepted performance-based pay.
- [ ] End product meets the standard regardless of tools used.

### Incentive lock template (before delivering value)

- What I deliver: ___
- What they deliver: ___
- Upside mechanism: equity ___% / revshare ___% / affiliate cut ___% / referral fee ___
- Trigger and payment timing: ___
- Ownership of assets created: ___
- Exit terms: ___

### Cold outreach message structure

1. Why this specific person (evidence you know their situation).
2. Their benefit, stated first.
3. One-line proof (case study or published work).
4. Minimal ask.

## Anti-patterns

- Guaranteed monthly retainers for contributors (EP learned this the hard way).
- Helping someone professionally without locking the incentive first.
- Paying the 4x personal-brand premium for a landing page or design.
- Hiring an agency as a buyer when in-house or direct freelancers are cheaper and more efficient.
- Going deeper technically than needed to spec logic for developers.
- Avoiding hiring to protect margins once profitable.
- Calls and meetings by default.
- Business-model hopping before the 1-2 month validation window.
- Public feuds with competitors.
