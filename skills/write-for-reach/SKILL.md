# Write for reach

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal laws.

Writing is the highest-ROI skill because almost every other valuable skill is an extension of it: process an idea, put it into words, format it so it makes sense. This skill takes a raw idea and runs it through EP's reach pipeline - a zero-context optimistic hook, maximally simple language, formatting treated like product UX, and a deliberate choice of format and platform. Core thesis: for reach, packaging beats substance, and simplicity is the distribution advantage rather than a compromise.

## When to use

- User has an idea, insight, or braindump and wants a post/thread/article that gets seen.
- User has a draft that is accurate but dense, technical, or mechanism-first and wants it to perform.
- User asks for hooks, titles, first lines, thumbnails, or "how do I open this".
- User asks "text or video?", "should this be a thread or a YouTube video?", "where should I post this?".
- User asks why a good piece of content flopped.
- User is deciding how much effort to put into a piece relative to its likely reach.

## Core principles

**Writing is the highest-ROI skill; practice it in public.** EP: two years of public writing dramatically improved how he formulates thoughts. Learn a thing, write about it so others understand, optimize for value and clarity - the practice sharpens the thinking, not just the output.

**Dumb it down - simplicity is the distribution advantage.** The more complex a post, the less reach. Readers scroll at the first hint of mental resistance, and the algorithm punishes scroll-pasts. EP's rule: assume every reader is a distracted, dyslexic idiot. Shorter sentences, simpler words, clear formatting. If a 10-year-old couldn't read it, it won't perform. Don't tailor to an experience level - write principles that apply at every level in maximally simple language.

**Packaging beats substance for reach.** The same content with a mass-appeal, outcome-first title consistently beats accurate mechanism-first framing. "$1000/day method for beginners" beats "how to effectively funnel traffic to your email list from short-form content"; "this prompt will change your life" reliably pulls 100k+ impressions where a sober description gets far less. Wrap the value in a mass-appeal hook; let the substance sit underneath.

**Hook formula: zero context + optimistic promise.** EP's tested opener "this thread will change your life" pulled 29k+ views. Mechanism: no cognitive load plus a big implied payoff. The reader shouldn't need to know anything to feel the pull.

**Inject the value proposition instantly.** A few seconds of dwell time decide viral vs flop. The reader must understand what they get from the post immediately, with no room for interpretation. Nobody will work to decode your offer.

**Structure is a multiplier; treat formatting like product UX.** The most life-changing insight in a wall of text fails. The average tweet gets about one second of dwell time (X is a lower-attention platform than TikTok), so line breaks, sentence length, and visual scannability decide whether the reader stays. Chopped, line-broken text also occupies more screen, increasing read-through; long text embedded in an image drives high view-time on media.

**Reach scales with total addressable audience.** EP's mega-viral prompts targeted the widest possible desire - people who want to change their lives. Niche-precise content trades ceiling for conversion quality; mass-desire content does the opposite. Before writing, ask: how many people is this even relevant to?

**Gate the technical sauce.** Timeline content should be instantly graspable "dopamine hits with no input lag". Genuinely technical material goes behind a lead magnet where subscribers consume it deliberately.

**Short-form is a volume game; long-form is a quality game.** Spam and iterate on short-form. Polish and invest in long-form, where one video keeps paying for a year.

**Judge content by ROI of effort versus impressions.** A tweet written in 2 minutes that gets 50k impressions beats an Instagram reel that took an hour for 50k views - though for similar content, long-form video is worth more per view because it is higher-intent attention. For reach per unit of effort, text on X is extremely efficient.

**X originates, short-form platforms broadcast.** Great thinkers are on X; great broadcasters are on IG/TikTok/YouTube. Ideas that originate on X can be repurposed into short-form for other platforms (EP: six-figure months this way; faceless educational reels were performing extremely well in 2025).

**Text beats video for info content about 80% of the time.** Text is easier to skim, structure, revise, and drop into an LLM. Reserve video for the ~20% that is hands-on UI navigation (n8n tutorials, design, media buying). EP: ninety percent of educational YouTube videos compress into a six-line tweet.

**Text filters, visuals scale.** Written marketing works in smart-money circles (X, newsletters) because comprehension requires baseline intelligence and interest. For mass appeal go visual - a child instantly understands an image; the masses will never parse a screenshot of a prompt.

## Workflow

1. **Capture the raw idea.** Get it in one or two sentences from the user, or extract it from their draft. Ask: what does the reader walk away able to do today? If the answer is "feel inspired", push for something actionable and step-by-step - EP's checklist requires value that is actionable, not vague inspiration.

2. **Size the audience.** Ask how many people this is relevant to. If it's narrow, decide deliberately: either widen the framing to a mass desire (higher ceiling, lower conversion quality) or keep it niche and accept lower reach with better conversion. State the trade-off to the user.

3. **Write the hook.** Apply zero context + optimistic promise. Draft 3-5 outcome-first openers. Prefer the version that a reader with no context would still feel pulled by. Check: does the value proposition land in the first line with no room for interpretation? If the hook explains a mechanism, rewrite it to promise an outcome.

4. **Dumb the body down.** Rewrite for a distracted 10-year-old: one idea per sentence, simple words, no jargon, no nested clauses. Cut anything the reader has to decode. If a section is genuinely technical, move it out of the post and into a lead magnet reference ("reply X for the full breakdown" style), keeping only the graspable summary in the timeline post.

5. **Format like UX.** One idea per line. Blank line between beats. Lists as short bullets. Lead with the promise, deliver steps, close with a single takeaway. For X, remember ~1 second of dwell time: the first two lines carry everything. If the content is long, consider embedding the text in an image to drive media view-time.

6. **Choose format and platform.**
   - If the idea is a principle, framework, or list: text on X first (highest reach per effort), then broadcast to short-form on IG/TikTok/YouTube.
   - If it requires showing a UI, hands-on process, or visual navigation: video.
   - If the target is a mass/beginner audience: go visual (image, whiteboard thumbnail, short-form video).
   - If the target is a sophisticated/smart-money audience: text plus extractable snippets.
   - If it's a long-form piece: invest in quality; if short-form: ship fast, iterate, post more.

7. **Run the three-point pre-post checklist** (below). If any point fails, fix it before posting.

8. **Post and observe.** Note what the hook did versus the body. Keep the hooks that work in a swipe file (see `direct-response-copywriting`).

## Checklists / templates

### Three-point pre-post checklist (EP, 2025)

1. You find the topic genuinely interesting in the moment.
2. You believe people will find it valuable - actionable and step-by-step, something they can start doing today, not vague inspiration.
3. It is formatted cleanly enough that a 12-year-old could read it.

### Hook rewrite pattern

| Mechanism-first (loses) | Outcome-first, zero context (wins) |
|---|---|
| how to effectively funnel traffic to your email list from short-form content | $1000/day method for beginners |
| a prompt for structured research on customer pain points | this prompt will change your life |
| a thread on formatting for dwell time | this thread will change your life |

### Before/after rewrite example (built from EP's own examples)

**Before (accurate, mechanism-first, dense):**

> How to effectively funnel traffic to your email list from short-form content: the mechanism is that short-form platforms are pattern-interrupt environments, so once you have snapped the viewer out of the doomscrolling trance you need to redirect them off-platform to an owned channel while you still have their attention, ideally via a lead magnet that gates the more technical material.

**After (packaged for reach):**

> $1000/day method for beginners
>
> short-form is a pattern interrupt
>
> your only job:
> - snap them out of the scroll
> - send them off-platform while you have them
>
> give them a free lead magnet for their email
>
> the technical stuff lives in the magnet, not the timeline
>
> that's the whole model

What changed: outcome-first hook with zero context; one idea per line; every sentence readable by a 10-year-old; the technical explanation is gated behind the lead magnet rather than in the post.

### Format/platform decision table

| Content type | Default format | Why (EP) |
|---|---|---|
| Principle, framework, list, insight | Text on X, then repurpose to short-form | Highest reach per minute of effort; X originates, others broadcast |
| Hands-on UI process (n8n, design, media buying) | Video | The ~20% where video beats text |
| Info product / course lesson | Text + markdown per lesson | Buyers feed it to an LLM; text is skimmable and structured |
| Mass / beginner audience | Visual, short-form, whiteboard thumbnail | Visuals scale; the masses won't parse a prompt screenshot |
| Sophisticated audience | Text plus extractable snippets | Text filters for intelligence and interest |
| Evergreen deep content | Long-form, polished | One video pays for a year |

## Anti-patterns

- Writing to an experience level ("for intermediate marketers") instead of principles at maximal simplicity.
- Leading with the mechanism or the accurate description instead of the promise.
- Walls of text. One insight buried in an unbroken paragraph fails regardless of quality.
- Making the reader infer the value. If it takes work to decode, they scroll.
- Spending an hour on a reel that could have been a two-minute tweet with the same impressions.
- Putting technical sauce in the timeline. It belongs in the lead magnet.
- Making video for content that is really a six-line tweet.
- Optimizing a niche piece for mass reach and then being surprised the leads don't convert (or vice versa) - pick the trade-off on purpose.
- Vague inspiration. The checklist requires "start doing today".
