# Funnels & Owned Channels

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill designs a funnel for a given offer and traffic source. Core thesis: platform ad revenue is a rounding error; the same eyeballs are worth 10-100x when routed through an owned channel (email list or Telegram) into your own offers. Discovery happens on socials, conversion happens in channels you control, and every view should be monetized from day one.

## When to use

- The user has traffic (or a plan for traffic) and no clear path from view to sale.
- The user is deciding whether to build for ad revenue, YouTube automation payouts, or X creator payouts.
- The user sends traffic straight to a sales page and wonders why conversion is ~1%.
- The user asks which channel (email, Telegram, SEO, YouTube, X, Reels, Shorts) to prioritize.
- The user is choosing a lead magnet, or their auto-DM delivery broke at scale.
- The user wants to know when a promotional format is "dead" and should be replaced.
- The user asks about sponsored-post pricing or how much an X audience is worth.
- The user wants a passive/evergreen setup (YouTube + email automations).
- The user is deciding between paid ads and organic for a product type.

## Core principles

**Never build for ad revenue - treat payouts as a bonus for doing your own marketing.** EP: monetizing views with your own product is worth 10-50x ad revenue (a channel averaging 2k views/day earns ~$10/day in ads but made $200/day selling to that audience), and directing X traffic into a solid offer earns 50-100x the payout. YouTube automation creators celebrating $1-2k per 500k views are leaving 50x on the table.

**The X account is a deal-flow asset, not a payout machine.** EP projected over $1M/year from Twitter leads versus ~$50k/year from ad revenue at the same activity level; most of his account's income comes from partnerships formed in DMs. Design the account to push traffic to funnels and offers first, payouts second.

**Route traffic through a list or community before the sales page.** EP's math: if 1% converts on a direct page, 99% are lost forever. If 20% join your list and 10% of those eventually buy, you make ~4x more from identical traffic and keep the ability to re-contact everyone. He says he "wasted literally millions of visitors" before learning this.

**Monetize from the very first views - never "build the audience first."** Revenue has nothing to do with follower count (EP converted a sale through a YouTube channel with 6 subscribers). Every unmonetized view is wasted. If a project gets no sale within the first few thousand views, kill it or change the approach.

**Kill-or-iterate at ~5,000 views.** If a short-form video promoting a low-ticket product hits ~5,000 views with no sale (or no set number of opt-ins), that format is dead. Cycle: one short-form style, then another, then long-form, then selling directly on X. Failure teaches more than brainstorming.

**Email is underrated; test Telegram too.** EP: ~30% of his digital product sales come from email, with a 55% open rate (2025-01). But his Telegram channel converts better than his email list in his niche, and YouTube Shorts funnel well into Telegram. Pick by testing, not by conventional wisdom.

**Rank channels by buyer intent, not volume.** EP's order (2025-08), highest to lowest: SEO, YouTube search, newsletter, Telegram channel, YouTube recommended, X, Instagram Reels/TikTok, YouTube Shorts. Search and owned channels convert best per view; short-form feeds give volume at the lowest intent.

**Ephemeral traffic must land in a newsletter, never on a one-shot offer.** EP once sent 1M clicks to a free-Robux CPA offer; when the method got patched he had nothing left. Owned capture converts spikes into a durable asset.

**Benchmark on a daily acquisition number into an owned channel: 100 new people into your Telegram channel every day.** EP calls this a concrete quit-your-job benchmark - not a vanity follower count.

**Pair traffic type to product type: paid traffic for physical products, organic for digital.** Digital margins survive organic volatility; physical unit economics can absorb paid acquisition. EP made 7 figures purely organic without running an ad, and says paid ads are best used to collect leads, not sell directly.

**Build a working tool as the lead magnet, not another PDF.** EP's example: a free tweet-generation app where users plug in their own API key (zero running cost) and must join the newsletter to sign up. A functional tool converts and differentiates far better than an ebook.

**Consider selling the lead magnet for ~$15 to filter info-hoarders.** Not for revenue - pulling out a card signals intent to execute. EP calls this a way to "100x the value of leads."

**Evergreen YouTube + automated email compounds for years.** EP's years-old videos still produce sales on products he'd forgotten; a couple of videos about one product made ~$1,000/month passively for months.

## Workflow

Walk the user through these steps. Ask for the offer, price point, traffic source(s), and current numbers first.

1. **Classify the offer.** Digital (course, playbook, community, SaaS, service) or physical? If digital: default to organic traffic. If physical: paid traffic is acceptable, but still capture leads rather than selling cold. If the user has no offer yet, stop and route to `info-products-sell-transformation` - a funnel without an offer is wasted views.

2. **Classify the traffic source by intent.** Place it on EP's ladder (SEO > YouTube search > newsletter > Telegram > YouTube recommended > X > Reels/TikTok > Shorts). High-intent sources can point closer to a sales page; low-intent, high-volume sources (Shorts, TikTok slideshows, viral spikes) must go to an opt-in.

3. **Pick the owned channel.** Default to email list AND a Telegram channel; test which converts in this niche. If the platform suppresses Telegram links, use YouTube as a link cloak (Telegram link in a video description, share the YouTube link). On X, external links are fine - put the link under the tweet.

4. **Design the lead magnet.** Prefer a working tool over a PDF. If a PDF, make it genuinely high-quality and directly tied to the offer. Decide: free, or a ~$15 token price to filter for executors (use the paid version when the back end is high-ticket and lead quality matters more than list size). Deliver via email-list signup, not auto-DM - if auto-DM breaks at scale, "subscribe and receive it in your inbox" converts the spike into subscribers anyway.

5. **Lay out the standard info funnel.** Fill in the diagram below with the user's specifics. Each tier qualifies buyers for the next.

6. **Build the automation.** Email sequence ending in a "Buy Now" CTA; daily value emails; one pitch per week. For evergreen: create product → lead magnet + clean opt-in page → automatic email sequence → ~100 evergreen videos funneling to the opt-in → post and forget.

7. **Set the kill-or-iterate thresholds before posting.** Define: sale-or-opt-in target per ~5,000 views of promo content; daily opt-in target (EP's benchmark: 100/day into Telegram). If the first few thousand views produce zero sales, change format or approach rather than "building audience."

8. **Layer in secondary revenue only after the funnel works.** Sponsored posts (EP's benchmarks: $1,000-$2,000 per tweet to mid-sized accounts, ~$2,500 for larger, $1k-$5k in all-in AI-niche accounts, 2026-01) and platform payouts are bonuses. Only promote what you actually use - off-brand deals erode the trust that makes the account valuable (EP turned down ~$200k of promos in a year).

9. **Review the metrics checklist** below on a weekly cadence and cycle formats when a threshold fails.

## Checklists / templates

### Funnel diagram (fill in per offer)

```
[Discovery - social content]
  Platform(s): ______  (intent tier: ___)
  Content promise: pain → value → soft CTA
          |
          v
[Lead magnet / opt-in]
  Type: working tool | high-quality PDF | ($15 token-priced filter?)
  Delivery: email signup (never auto-DM only)
          |
          v
[Owned channel]
  Email list (daily value, weekly pitch)  +  Telegram channel (test which converts)
          |
          v
[Email automation → "Buy Now" CTA]
          |
          v
[Low-ticket front end]  $15-$50 playbook / $17 impulse guide / paid community
          |
          v
[High-ticket back end]  mentorship, done-with-you, service, B2B
```

### Metrics checklist

- [ ] Sales in the first few thousand views? If zero, kill or change approach.
- [ ] Promo short-form: at least 1 sale or N opt-ins per ~5,000 views? If not, format is dead - cycle.
- [ ] Daily new opt-ins into owned channel (target: 100/day into Telegram as the quit-your-job benchmark).
- [ ] Opt-in rate from traffic (EP's illustrative target: ~20% join list vs ~1% direct-page conversion).
- [ ] Share of sales from email vs Telegram vs timeline (EP: ~30% email; Telegram outperformed email in his niche; most launch sales came from Telegram).
- [ ] Email open rate (EP cites 55% as evidence email isn't dead).
- [ ] Which format currently converts: short-form style A / B / long-form / direct X selling?
- [ ] Ad revenue and sponsorships tracked separately as a bonus line, never the plan.

### Channel-pairing quick reference

| Situation | Route |
|---|---|
| Viral spike / TikTok slideshows | → newsletter opt-in, never a one-shot offer |
| YouTube Shorts | → Telegram (EP: funnels well) |
| X | → link under tweet to Telegram/opt-in (X is off-platform-link friendly) |
| Platform suppresses Telegram links | → YouTube video description as link cloak |
| Digital product | → organic traffic |
| Physical product | → paid traffic acceptable; still collect leads |

## Anti-patterns

- Building a channel whose business model is the payout. EP's X benchmarks are ~$0.03-$0.05 RPM; a six-figure-earning account collected ~$1,500 lifetime ad revenue.
- "Build the audience first, monetize later." Every unmonetized view is wasted; follower count is unrelated to revenue.
- Direct-to-sales-page traffic with no capture step (loses 99%).
- Sending a viral spike to an affiliate/CPA offer that can be patched.
- Taking off-brand sponsorships because the rate card is attractive.
- Relying on auto-DM delivery at scale without an email fallback.
- Payout arbitrage schemes (EP describes the Spotify/DistroKid bought-streams template; it is patched and ToS-violating - don't run it). Ragebait persona pages farming engagement payouts - EP presents it to explain the timeline, "not a recommended model."
- Running paid ads to sell digital products directly instead of collecting leads.
