# Market and Problem Selection

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill helps you pick a problem worth solving and validate that a paying community exists before building. Core thesis: your best ideas come from problems you have personally faced or that your network exposes you to; boring, passionate, or local niches with weak marketing and high customer value beat flashy models that influencers promote; and no idea is valid until there is proof of a community constantly discussing the problem and money changing hands.

## When to use

- The user has no product idea, or too many, and needs a selection procedure.
- The user is about to enter a flashy, heavily promoted model.
- The user is building for their own peer group (indie hackers building for indie hackers) without evidence those buyers have money.
- The user asks whether a niche is "too saturated" or "too small".
- The user wants tools or methods to research what already sells.
- The user is choosing between online and physical/local business.

## Core principles

**Solve problems you have personally faced.** EP calls this the principle he returns to most; the majority of his most successful ventures productized solutions to his own problems. Having lived the problem tells you what to build, how to market it, and where sufferers look.

**No ideas means no problems.** Start any business and you hit 100 real problems in the first month. Indie hackers only build for indie hackers because those are the only problems their network exposes them to.

**Your network determines which problems you see.** EP's mechanical reading of "network is net worth": befriend wealthy people, learn their problems, solve one, productize it. Poor-people problems can only be sold to poor people.

**Avoid the flashy models everyone promotes.** Trading, dropshipping, YouTube automation, SMMA, copywriting have a thousand times more supply than demand because influencers with rented Lamborghinis market them. If something looks like THE way to make money, run the other way. Companies selling egg cartons do nine figures.

**Niche down ruthlessly.** For a ~$10k/month goal, be #1 in a hyper-specific niche: easier to be the #1 safari tour reviewer in Tanzania than the #1000 travel YouTuber. Expert in one domain, generalist in all others. EP earned a surgeon's salary ranking YouTube videos for one keyword at ~2k views/day.

**Boring local businesses are underserved and high-value.** Dentists and plumbers have weak marketing and high customer value. EP's benchmark (2025-09): a Whop seller charges dentists $3,000/month for patient-acquisition marketing; 20 members is ~$60k/month. Trade-off: a scale ceiling.

**Any passionate community works; you don't need a money niche.** BillionGraves (grave-indexing for genealogists) reached an estimated $5M-$20M; 10+ Pokémon-card Whop communities each do four to five figures a day.

**Sell shovels to crowds that can't monetize.** When practitioners flood a market with no barrier to entry (95% of fitness coaches earn nothing), the profitable layer is teaching them how to monetize.

**Build a better version of what you already consume.** Heavy consumption is domain familiarity, an unfair advantage in product design. For mass-market B2C, deliberately live as an ordinary consumer.

**Job listings are proven business ideas.** Nobody hires at a loss. A salaried role is evidence of a profitable activity you can productize or sell as a service.

**No community discussing the problem = not an important problem.** Embrace one niche, talk to people making real money in it, and you will compile 100+ software-solvable inefficiencies. Require solid proof before building.

**Follow where the largest players commit capital.** Tech giants never raced on crypto; all race on AI. Structural competition among giants is the durability test for a trend.

**Nothing is too saturated, but choose your arena.** Saturation objections usually mask a lack of differentiation. Counterweight: if your competition is kids farming sales off a Google Doc, you are in the wrong arena. Physical businesses are often profitable simply because barriers keep competitors out.

## Workflow

1. **Inventory personal problems and exposure.** List (a) problems the user has personally solved or suffers from, (b) problems their wealthiest contacts or clients complain about, (c) what the user consumes most (content, tools, hobbies), (d) any industry they have worked inside. Decision point: if every candidate comes from the "indie hacker / make money online" bucket, push the user to widen exposure: pick a boring industry, join its communities, or take a role near wealthy operators.

2. **Generate candidates from external evidence.** Add ideas from: job listings and hiring posts (what are companies paying salaries for?), boring-industry top companies (their biggest expenses and clunky processes), passionate hobby communities, crowds of practitioners who can't monetize, and what large players are committing capital to.

3. **Kill flashy models.** Strike any candidate that is currently promoted by influencers as THE way to make money, unless the user has a real edge rooted in experience.

4. **Niche each survivor down.** Rewrite each candidate as "[specific outcome] for [hyper-specific group] in [specific context]" until the user could plausibly become the #1 provider within a year.

5. **Validate demand.** For each: find at least one community constantly discussing the problem; find at least one product or service already earning from it; talk to 3-5 people making real money in the niche. Use research tools (see checklist). Decision point: no community and no paying competitor → discard, do not "educate the market".

6. **Score with the rubric.** Rank candidates. Pick the top one; keep the runner-up as a fallback.

7. **Confirm fit and barrier.** Does the user have or can they acquire (or partner for) domain depth? Is there a barrier to entry (specialized knowledge, physical presence, network) that keeps Google-Doc copycats out? If the niche is purely open-internet with no edge, either add an edge or reconsider.

8. **Commit for a validation window.** Give the chosen niche 1-2 months of real effort (offer in front of traffic) before judging. Route to `offer-design-and-buyer-psychology` and `distribution-first-strategy` for the next steps.

## Checklists / templates

### Niche scoring rubric (1-5 each, total out of 45)

| Criterion | 1 | 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Lived experience | Never touched the problem | Personally solved it repeatedly |
| Buyer money | Buyers are broke | Buyers have budget; customer value is high |
| Community evidence | No one discusses it online | Constant discussion in identifiable places |
| Proof of payment | No one sells anything for it | Multiple products/services earning |
| Specificity | Broad category | Hyper-specific, ownable within a year |
| Marketing weakness of incumbents | Slick, well-funded marketers | Weak or absent marketing (boring/local) |
| Barrier to entry | Anyone with a Google Doc | Specialized knowledge, network, or physical presence |
| Flashiness (reverse-scored) | Influencer-promoted model | Nobody promotes it |
| Capital commitment of big players | Fading trend | Structural investment by largest companies |

Interpretation: below 25 discard; 25-34 needs an edge before proceeding; 35+ proceed to offer design.

### Validation checkpoints (all must pass)

- [ ] A community that constantly discusses the problem exists (name it).
- [ ] At least one competitor demonstrably earns from it (name it, estimate revenue if possible).
- [ ] 3-5 conversations with people making real money in the niche produced a list of inefficiencies.
- [ ] The user can describe the sufferer's pain in their own words.
- [ ] The user knows where sufferers search for solutions.

### Research tool stack (EP, 2025)

- Whop Pulse (free): what is actively selling and searched on Whop; high search volume = validated buying intent.
- TrustMRR: a product's revenue.
- Swipebuilder: a product's ad creatives.
- Pair TrustMRR + Swipebuilder to reverse-engineer a proven offer end to end.
- Job boards and niche Discord hiring posts: message hirers and work out why hiring you would make them money.

### Idea sources worksheet

1. My own recurring problems: ___
2. Problems my richest contacts mention: ___
3. What I consume most and could do better: ___
4. Roles companies are paying for in [industry]: ___
5. Boring-industry processes that are clunky: ___
6. Practitioners who can't monetize in [market]: ___
7. Passionate hobby communities I belong to: ___

## Anti-patterns

- Building novelties for broke users while someone less technical solves real problems for people with money.
- Picking a model because an influencer says it is lucrative.
- Chronic generalism; never going deep enough in one niche to build authority.
- Treating "saturated" as a reason to quit instead of a prompt to differentiate.
- Entering a zero-barrier arena (faceless info Telegram funnels full of copycats) without real, experience-rooted sauce.
- Taking a guru's word for where money flows instead of checking what creators sell and which ads perform.
