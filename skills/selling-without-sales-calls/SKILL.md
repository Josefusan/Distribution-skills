# Selling Without Sales Calls

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill produces a DM-close script skeleton and a landing page checklist. Core thesis: the public hates sales calls. A visible "buy now" button can outperform a booked-call funnel; nothing under ~$2k should need a call; high-ticket should close in ~10 DMs; and the best cold DM is a plain statement of what you do and exactly how it benefits the recipient. EP earned seven figures without a single sales call or VSL.

## When to use

- The user is designing a checkout or booking flow for an info product, community, or service.
- The user wants to write cold or warm DMs, especially to B2B or sophisticated buyers.
- A prospect asked the price and the user is unsure whether to state it.
- The user's funnel has a long VSL or application-call step in front of a sub-$2k offer.
- The user is spending 30+ minutes per prospect convincing them.
- The user wants the simplest possible setup to start selling.

## Core principles

**A buy-now button beats a booked call for anything under ~$2k.** EP: "as a buyer I won't book a call for anything under ~$2k either." Calls add friction the public resents; he earned seven figures without taking a single sales call or making a VSL.

**When a prospect asks the price, state it. "Let's hop on a call" gets you blocked.** Dodging the number reads as a trap; stating it reads as confidence.

**High-ticket should close in a short DM exchange (~10 messages).** Needing a 30-minute convincing session signals the offer or messaging is weak. The better the product and messaging, the less persuasion is required - if you're hard-convincing, fix the offer, not the script.

**The best cold DM is a plain statement of what you do and how it benefits the recipient.** EP's exact form: "hey, I do XYZ, {how it benefits you}" beats every script. Obvious sales tactics kill B2B deals with sophisticated buyers; part of the job is judging whether the prospect will see through canned techniques.

**State your non-negotiable price up front before explaining scope.** It filters time-wasters expecting the offer at 10% of its worth. It costs some raw conversion but saves enormous back-and-forth.

**Match funnel aggressiveness to traffic temperature.** Warm organic traffic: plain copy and a buy-now button; the emotional persuasion was already done by your content, and a 60-minute unskippable VSL only adds friction. Cold traffic in markets like healthcare or insurance: long VSLs still work.

**Minimum viable stack: landing page + buy-now button + traffic.** Don't overbuild funnels before proving traffic converts on the simplest possible page.

**Faceless works.** EP sold many types of digital products without showing his face, taking a call, or making a VSL - landing page, content-driven traffic, and self-serve checkout replace personal branding.

## Workflow

Gather: the offer, price, whether traffic is warm (your content) or cold (outbound/ads), buyer sophistication (consumer vs operator/B2B).

1. **Route by price.** Under ~$2k: no call, ever. Buy-now button on a landing page; DMs only to answer questions. $2k and up: DM close targeting ~10 messages; offer a call only if the buyer requests one, never as a gate.

2. **Route by traffic temperature.** Warm (they consumed your content): plain copy, proof, buy-now. Cold (outbound/ads in healthcare/insurance-style markets): a long VSL is acceptable; otherwise still prefer plain copy. Do not put a VSL in front of warm traffic.

3. **Build the minimum stack first.** Landing page (EP's baseline: build with bolt.new) + buy-now via Whop (or Gumroad) + traffic. Only add email sequences, upsells, and BNPL after the simple page has produced sales. See `funnels-and-owned-channels` for the capture layer.

4. **Write the DM opener.** Use the plain-statement template below. Strip every recognizable "technique" (fake compliments, loom-video bait, "quick question?"). For sophisticated buyers, assume they've seen every script.

5. **Handle the price ask.** State the number in the next message. Then scope. Never "let's hop on a call" as the answer to "how much?"

6. **Close in ~10 messages.** Use the skeleton below. If you're past 10 and still convincing, note the objection - it's a messaging or offer defect to fix on the page, not a reason to push harder.

7. **Send the buy link, not a calendar link.** For high-ticket, use a checkout link (Whop supports high-ticket checkout links and BNPL). Follow up once; then let the content keep warming them.

8. **Review the landing page against the checklist** and confirm it deviates from the standard info-product template (sameness pattern-matches to scam).

## Checklists / templates

### Cold DM template (EP's exact form)

```
hey, I do XYZ, {how it benefits you}
```

Filled example structure (replace brackets; keep it one plain sentence):

```
hey, I do [what you do, in the words a peer would use], [the specific benefit to this recipient - a number, an outcome, or a removed headache].
```

Rules: no compliment opener, no "quick question", no mystery. State price if they ask, or lead with it for high-ticket.

### Price-first variant (high-ticket, sophisticated buyers)

```
hey, I do [XYZ] for [type of business] - [benefit]. It's $[non-negotiable price]. If that's in range, here's how it works: [2-3 lines of scope].
```

Why lead with price: it filters people expecting the offer at 10% of its worth and saves the back-and-forth.

### ~10-message DM close skeleton

```
1. You:   plain statement (template above)
2. Them:  interest / "what does that involve?" / "how much?"
3. You:   price (if not already stated) + one-line scope
4. Them:  question or objection
5. You:   direct answer + one proof point (a result, not a screenshot wall)
6. Them:  second question / fit check
7. You:   answer; state who it is NOT for (qualification)
8. Them:  "ok how do we start?"
9. You:   checkout link (BNPL option noted for high-ticket) + what happens after payment
10. Them: paid / "let me think"
   You:   if "think": one follow-up later; no pressure sequence.
```

Decision point: if a "call" is requested by the buyer, take it - but never require it. If you're still convincing at message 10, stop and fix the offer/page.

### Landing page checklist (warm traffic, no VSL)

- [ ] Plain copy stating the transformation (see `info-products-sell-transformation`).
- [ ] Price visible; no "apply to learn pricing" for anything under ~$2k.
- [ ] Prominent buy-now button (Whop/Gumroad checkout).
- [ ] BNPL enabled on high-ticket.
- [ ] Layout deviates from the standard info-product template.
- [ ] No unskippable long VSL for warm traffic (allowed only for cold traffic in VSL-tolerant markets).
- [ ] Not called a "course."
- [ ] No promised income figures (see `ethical-selling-and-grifter-detection`).
- [ ] Works without a face or personal brand (self-serve checkout).
- [ ] Instrumented (EP uses PostHog session replays to watch where people drop).

## Anti-patterns

- Gating a sub-$2k offer behind a booked call or application.
- Answering "how much?" with "let's hop on a call."
- Scripted DM openers with recognizable tactics sent to sophisticated buyers.
- 30-minute convincing sessions - a symptom of a weak offer.
- A 60-minute unskippable VSL in front of warm organic traffic.
- Building multi-step funnels before a plain page has converted.
- Hiding the price to "get them on the phone."
