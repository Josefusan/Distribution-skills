# Online Business Playbooks

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill selects the playbook that fits the user's constraints and walks it as a checklist. Core thesis: 99% of online businesses follow one universal template (problem → solution → productize → find where sufferers search → position content there → funnel to owned channel), and each named playbook is that template specialized for a starting position: no skills, no capital, no face, no code, or an existing skill to package.

## When to use

- The user wants a complete sequence, not a single tactic.
- The user states constraints: "I can't code", "I have $0", "I won't show my face", "I have no audience".
- The user wants to start an AI-based business from scratch.
- The user has a skill (automation, content, AI media) and wants a path to clients.
- The user wants a repeatable idea-generation or competitor-research system.
- The user is unsure whether to build SaaS, info, or services.

## Core principles

**The universal template.** Encounter a problem many share → solve it → productize (DWY/DFY, info, software) → find where sufferers search → position content at those touchpoints → funnel to a landing page and email list. Compact indie version: fix your own problem, post how you fixed it, build an audience with the same problem, commercialize. The fix is proof; the audience is pre-qualified.

**Niche expertise comes before any AI work.** In EP's AI-from-scratch playbook, step 2 is diving deep into the industry; the AI solution is chosen for marketability, not ease, and the sales page is envisioned before building.

**Don't sell until you have an audience.** Post content solving the OTHER pain points on your list, with a newsletter alongside so no traffic is wasted; selling becomes natural once the vertical trusts you for solutions.

**If you can't code, don't vibe-code it.** Build distribution first, then pitch; cracked devs line up to partner with someone who has distribution locked.

**Write down which problems you hit.** The "$0 no skills" playbook is built on actually attempting a model for a few weeks purely to document real problems, then learning automation to solve those documented problems.

**Product type follows constraints.** SaaS scales best, hardest to build and sell; info scales second-best (make once, sell forever; eroded by leaks and hype decay) and demands real knowledge and trust; services scale least and start easiest via cold outreach with case studies.

**Connect two parties valuable to each other.** With $0 and no skills you can still profit by brokering (X arbitrage, UGC flywheel). Managing a roster of creators out-earns selling advice to the same audience.

**Validate manually first, automate second.** EP's first Python script made $2,000/day after one day of tutorials because it automated an already-proven manual loop. Simple automation applied to a validated loop has instant ROI.

**AI is a 10x on each step, not the system.** The A-to-Z playbook assigns a tool to every step, but the fundamentals are the system.

## Workflow

1. **Capture constraints.** Ask or infer: Can you code? Capital available? Willing to show your face? Existing skill (automation, video, writing, AI media)? Existing audience? Personally-solved problem? Industry access?

2. **Select the playbook.**
   - Personally solved a money/freedom/status problem → A-to-Z AI execution.
   - No skills, no capital, willing to document publicly → $0 no-skills automation.
   - Want an AI business but no niche yet → Full AI-from-scratch.
   - Have a product idea, deciding type/distribution → Digital-product build-and-distribute.
   - Already running a business model and hitting problems → SaaS-with-built-in-distribution.
   - Can't code, have or can build distribution → Digital-product playbook step 3 (partner with a dev).
   - $0, no skills, comfortable with outreach and brokering → X arbitrage or UGC flywheel.
   - Skilled at AI media (image/video/sound) → AI content-service path.
   - Need ideas and have a peer → Idea-generation playbook.
   - Already posting content to promote a product → Add the competitor-scraping research layer.
   If two apply, run the one that produces a purchasable offer soonest.

3. **Walk the checklist.** Present the chosen playbook's steps as a checklist; convert each into a dated task with a concrete deliverable. Decision point at the validation step of every playbook: no community discussing the problem, or no proof people pay → back to `market-and-problem-selection`.

4. **Set the validation window.** 1-2 months of proper effort with an offer in front of traffic. Then double down or change one variable. Do not hop models before the window ends.

5. **Add the owned channel and the research layer.** Every playbook ends with educational content funnelling to email/Telegram where selling happens; add the competitor-scraping layer once content is the main acquisition engine.

6. **Route onward.** Pricing/packaging → `offer-design-and-buyer-psychology`; channel choice → `distribution-first-strategy`; hiring or partnership → `partnerships-hiring-delegation`.

## Checklists / templates

### Universal template

- [ ] Problem many people share (lived or observed)
- [ ] Working solution
- [ ] Productized form: DWY / DFY / info / software
- [ ] Where sufferers search for solutions (list)
- [ ] Content positioned at those touchpoints
- [ ] Landing page + email list funnel

### 1. Full AI-from-scratch playbook (EP, 2025-07)

1. [ ] Find a niche where money flows (SaaS, ecommerce, infoproducts, YouTube automation) with problems plausibly solvable by AI; follow the money (what creators sell, which ads perform); never take gurus' word.
2. [ ] Dive deep: study the industry, join free and paid communities, podcasts, follow niche people across platforms.
3. [ ] Ranked list of the most-mentioned pain points; multiple AI solutions per pain point; choose by marketability; envision the sales page before building.
4. [ ] Build with the community: free access, honest feedback, iterate.
5. [ ] Don't sell yet: post content showing AI solutions to the OTHER pain points, on the vertical's platform, newsletter alongside.
6. [ ] With trust in one vertical, sell; you own product and distribution.

### 2. A-to-Z AI execution playbook (EP, 2025-12)

1. [ ] Painful problem you personally solved, tied to money, freedom, or status.
2. [ ] Validate with GPT deep research: who faces it, competitors, reviews, complaints; confirm people pay.
3. [ ] Choose product type: software, info, or agency.
4. [ ] Have Claude build a context profile of the offer for reuse in future sessions.
5. [ ] Build with AI: Claude for course structure or service SOPs; Cursor for software.
6. [ ] Have Claude write sales-page copy from the context profile + a copywriting SOP + examples of good pages.
7. [ ] Landing page in Framer or vibe-coded with Lovable.
8. [ ] VSL: LLM copy loaded with best practices, ElevenLabs voice, Nano Banana + Veo 3.1 + CapCut visuals.
9. [ ] Payments via Stripe or Whop.
10. [ ] Find where the audience congregates; study the algorithm for a few days; list 10 creators the ICP follows; post genuinely valuable content about the problem; collaborate with those creators (disclosed sponsorships or partnerships) to speed initial reach.
11. [ ] Funnel to the landing page via bio links, replies, case-study posts.

### 3. "$0 with no skills" automation playbook (EP, 2025-04)

1. [ ] Pick a popular model (ecom, info, SaaS).
2. [ ] Study it for one week.
3. [ ] Attempt it for a few weeks; write down every problem hit.
4. [ ] Join a cheap paid or free community; learn which problems others hit.
5. [ ] Learn n8n-style workflow automation to solve those documented problems.
6. [ ] Set up X, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram documenting how you solve them.
7. [ ] Inbound: people with the same problems reach out; sign clients.

### 4. Digital-product build-and-distribute playbook (EP, 2025-09)

1. [ ] Pick product type by constraints: SaaS (scales best, hardest), info (make once, sell forever; needs knowledge, packaging, trust), services (least scalable, easiest; cold outreach with case studies).
2. [ ] Validate: communities constantly discussing the problem.
3. [ ] Can't code? Build distribution channels first, then pitch for a dev partner.
4. [ ] Proper payment platform (EP: Whop over Stripe).
5. [ ] Distribute inside the communities where you validated; reverse-engineer top players' content; if content needs a face you won't show, percentage partnerships with hungry creators.
6. [ ] Educational content → owned email/Telegram → hard-sell only there.

### 5. SaaS-with-built-in-distribution path (EP, 2025-04)

1. [ ] Run a business model.
2. [ ] Hit real problems.
3. [ ] Publicly document how you use AI to fix them.
4. [ ] Turn the solution into software.
5. [ ] Use the personal brand built while documenting as the distribution channel.

### 6. Idea-generation playbook (EP, 2025-03)

1. [ ] Partner on the same mission; chat daily, sharing what you learn.
2. [ ] Once a day, export the chat history to a folder.
3. [ ] Open in an AI IDE (e.g., Windsurf); have the agent clean it into a "YOU:/PARTNER:" markdown.
4. [ ] Prompt: "You are an expert business strategist and conversation analyst. Extract all the ideas discussed."
5. [ ] Follow-up prompt: "Rank the ideas by how lucrative they are and link any that are better combined."

### 7. X arbitrage playbook (EP, 2025-09), whitehat version

1. [ ] Find agency owners / service providers / gurus with audiences on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok but no X presence.
2. [ ] Line up established X accounts in their niche willing to collaborate (disclosed cross-promotion, reposts, or a revenue share as declared partners).
3. [ ] Pitch the influencer: you run their entire X presence for a percentage of all profits.
4. [ ] Use AI to extract top insights from their existing content and reformat into engaging tweets.
5. [ ] Partner accounts engage and share openly.
6. [ ] Once the account has momentum, promote the influencer's offer, optionally with a Whop community.

### 8. UGC flywheel (EP, 2025-09)

1. [ ] Partner with a UGC creator to make content about how they earn.
2. [ ] Sell a low-ticket community teaching people to become UGC creators.
3. [ ] Build a network of ecommerce brand owners.
4. [ ] Broker deals between brands and your creator network for a percentage.

### 9. AI content-service path (EP, 2025-04)

1. [ ] Learn to ideate and script with LLMs.
2. [ ] Get good at image, video, and sound generation (4o, Runway, Kling, ElevenLabs, Flux).
3. [ ] Build a distinctive portfolio.
4. [ ] Grow a personal brand along the way.
5. [ ] Sell the service to brands.

### 10. Validate manually first, automate second

1. [ ] Run the money loop by hand until it demonstrably pays.
2. [ ] Identify the repetitive bottleneck step.
3. [ ] Automate only that step with the simplest script or workflow.
4. [ ] Measure ROI; expand automation only on proven loops.

### 11. Competitor-scraping research layer (EP, 2026-08)

1. [ ] Scrape all content promoting products like yours.
2. [ ] Rank by performance, outliers, sales intent, and engagement ratio.
3. [ ] Store in a database your agent can query.
4. [ ] Use it for ideas, scripts, and automated content creation.

## Anti-patterns

- Selling before having an audience in the vertical.
- Vibe-coding a product when you have neither code skill nor distribution.
- Skipping the "attempt it and write down problems" phase and learning automation in the abstract.
- Building a business on a patchable exploit: EP's Bandicam/YouTube Shorts upload workaround made $2,000/day and is long patched; take only the "automate a validated loop" lesson.
- Buying engagement or coordinated boosting: EP's original A-to-Z step 10 and X-arbitrage step 2 describe paying accounts to engage or boost; that is manipulation under most platform rules. Use disclosed sponsorships, collaborations, and revenue-share partnerships instead.
- Hopping playbooks before the 1-2 month validation window ends.
