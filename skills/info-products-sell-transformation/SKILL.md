# Info Products: Sell Transformation, Not Information

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill structures a product ladder with pricing. Core thesis: people buy the pay-to-win route from their current state to a desired state, not information - so frame every product as a transformation, keep the front end cheap and stable as an acquisition tool, never sell static information above ~$500, and put the real margin in a back end (done-with-you, service, community-with-tools, B2B) that you can actually fulfil.

## When to use

- The user is creating or repositioning a course, ebook, playbook, cohort, community, or coaching offer.
- The user asks how to price, or whether to raise the price of, a digital product.
- The user's messaging describes features ("automate your outreach") instead of outcomes.
- The user sells in a saturated niche and thinks the moat is knowledge.
- The user is choosing between selling a service and selling info, or between retainer and revshare.
- The user asks what to do about AI making information free.
- The user wants a concrete revenue model ($100/day, first $10k, etc.).

## Core principles

**People buy transformations, not solutions.** EP: "go from zero to $10k/month" beats "automate your outreach"; software only becomes an easy sell with a get-rich-quick element. Supplements and digital products are marketed identically - by instilling belief in a life-changing outcome. Frame every product as the pay-to-win route from current state to desired state.

**In saturated niches, the product is identity reframing plus enforced accountability.** All fitness knowledge is in a free 20-minute YouTube video, yet 99% fail. Clients pay for reframing into an athlete identity, eating for fuel, daily training, religious data tracking. Package coaching around enforcement and check-ins ("transformation accountability groups"), not information.

**Real, instantly applicable methods are the edge.** Most courses are theory-heavy; a documented, practiced, immediately actionable process is the moat. EP: selling methods you haven't applied yourself is essentially scamming.

**Emotional angles outconvert neutral ones.** "Become faster/stronger than your teammates" far outconverted generic promotion of the same athlete program. People impulse-buy in emotional states. EP flags this as ethically double-edged - insecure audiences have extremely high purchase intent for anything offering an illusion of progress. Use the emotion to sell a real transformation, not to exploit (see `ethical-selling-and-grifter-detection`).

**For men, map the offer to survival or reproduction:** status, safety, money, or attractiveness - never abstract features.

**Trends supply fresh "mechanisms."** A credible new angle (EP's example: the peptide trend in fitness) restores hope in prospects who already failed with mainstream paths and re-opens the sale. Watch new research and novel angles.

**Position between an existing belief and its believers rather than creating demand.** Large belief tribes (diet camps, etc.) already have millions of followers; when your advice lines up with what they believe, they gravitate to you.

**Don't say "course."** Use "workshop," "cohort," or "playbook." The word triggers a conditioned negative reaction. "$10k/month SWE accelerator" and "software engineering bachelor's degree" describe the same thing with opposite reception.

**Deviate from the standard landing page template.** Sameness pattern-matches to "scam" - viewers have clicked the identical layout 50 times. Breaking the pattern is itself a conversion advantage.

**Sell in the niche you actually know.** The #1 athletic performance mentor makes more than the #1000th sports betting tipster. Depth and position within a niche beat raw niche size.

**When AI has all the information, sell the lens.** Tools, curated context, and filters that encode judgment. An LLM always gives a correct answer but never the best one - which is why communities, consulting, courses, and mentorship stay sellable. AI coach apps trained on your material upgrade delivery while your proprietary knowledge stays the moat (2025-10).

**Low-ticket info is the front end; the real money is the back end.** Use $15-$50 products to acquire and qualify buyers, then ascend them to SaaS, high-ticket services, done-with-you, licensing, or B2B. In the AI wave, low-ticket courses surface and warm B2B leads (2025-04).

**Hold the front-end price low and stable.** A cheap front end makes the backend upsell look more attractive by contrast; a $27 offer only matters in light of the backend.

**Don't sell static info above ~$500.** Static-info buyers aren't pre-qualified and most never act, so premium prices for unaccompanied information are exploitative. EP's most ethical structure: low-ticket playbooks plus a high-ticket done-with-you offer; his coaching's 100% success rate comes from hand-held execution.

**Sequence services before info.** High-ticket done-for-you/done-with-you sells on results, pays far more per sale, and doesn't need months of audience-building; the info product later becomes a lead magnet for the service (2025-11). If you can't fulfil the service, you shouldn't sell the info either.

**Low-ticket at scale is not beneath you.** Hormozi did millions selling a $29 book via livestream. EP has done seven figures from low-ticket alone with a traffic engine feeding high-ticket. Farm $17 impulse purchases through short-form first; the guide's real job is getting buyers into your ecosystem.

**Cheap add-ons roughly double revenue.** Stack core product + community chat + educational material + embedded tools under one platform. Purely info-based communities are dead: teach people to solve a problem, put them among people solving it, give them tools that make it easier.

**Offer BNPL on high-ticket before optimizing anything else.** EP: ~30% of high-ticket sales on Iman Gadzhi's challenge Whop close through Splitit BNPL (2025-09).

**Prefer revenue share to flat fees when you can actually deliver.** A vendor who can genuinely 2x+ revenue would be irrational to take a $5k/month retainer; structure revshare (e.g., 33% of profit for 3x-ing revenue) or hybrid (fee + revshare). Caveat: only where you can move the whole revenue line, not commodity services.

**$100/day mental model:** create a $100 digital product and sell it once per day. Units-per-day of one offer makes goals concrete and testable.

**Build once, sell twice.** Prioritize zero-marginal-cost assets. Passive income always means someone or something working for the money: a $10k/month client fulfilled by a $5k/month hire is $5k of passive margin - delivery arbitrage, not magic.

## Workflow

Gather: niche, what the user has actually done/achieved in it, what they can fulfil personally, current audience/traffic, and target monthly revenue.

1. **Verify the niche and the skill.** Ask: have you applied this method yourself with results? If no, stop - selling unapplied methods is scamming per EP. Prefer the niche the user knows deeply over the one that looks lucrative.

2. **Define the transformation.** Write one sentence: "Go from [current state] to [desired state] in [timeframe] via [unique mechanism]." For men's offers, check it maps to status, safety, money, or attractiveness. Check for a fresh mechanism from a current trend. Identify the belief tribe whose existing convictions the offer aligns with.

3. **Choose the emotional angle.** Frame relative to peers ("faster than your teammates") rather than neutral. Then run the ethics check: is the transformation real and verifiable? If it relies on keeping buyers insecure or promising income, rework it.

4. **Decide the sequence: service first or info first?** If the user can fulfil a done-with-you/done-for-you service now, sell that first (results-based, no audience needed) and build the info product later as its lead magnet. If they already have traffic, start with the $17-$50 front end and add the service/community behind it.

5. **Build the ladder** using the template below. Front end $15-$50 (or a $17 impulse guide via short-form). Middle: paid community with core product + chat + educational material + embedded tools. Top: done-with-you, service, or B2B (whitelabeling, AI-integration) - never static info above ~$500. Add BNPL on the top tier.

6. **Name it.** Strip "course" from every name. Use workshop / cohort / playbook / accelerator / sprint / challenge / program.

7. **Design the landing page to break the template.** Reference `direct-response-copywriting` and `selling-without-sales-calls` for the page itself; here just ensure it does not look like the 50 pages the prospect already saw.

8. **Set the revenue model.** Translate the target to units-per-day of one offer (e.g., $100/day = one $100 product per day). Decide if any B2B deals should be revshare/hybrid rather than flat fee.

9. **Plan the AI upgrade.** Where can the user encode their judgment into a tool, curated context, or AI coach trained on their material so the offer sells "the lens" and stays sellable as raw information commoditizes?

## Checklists / templates

### Product ladder template

| Tier | Role | Price band (EP) | Contents | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead magnet | capture | free or ~$15 filter | tool or high-quality guide | see `funnels-and-owned-channels` |
| Front end | acquire + qualify | $15-$50 ($17 impulse; $27 example) | playbook / guide "how I did it" | keep low and stable; never the profit center |
| Community | retain + ascend | affordable subscription (EP's own: $75/month, info + tools) | core product + chat + education + embedded tools | never purely info; segment members |
| Back end | profit | high-ticket; BNPL enabled | done-with-you, service, B2B whitelabel, AI integration | only if you can fulfil; static info never > ~$500 |

### Transformation statement

```
From: ______________________ (current state / pain)
To:   ______________________ (desired state; survival/reproduction mapped if male audience)
In:   ______ (timeframe)      Via: ______________________ (unique mechanism / trend)
Aligned belief tribe: ______________   Peer-relative angle: ______________
Have I done this myself? [ ] yes (required)
```

### Naming check

- [ ] No "course" anywhere. Use workshop / cohort / playbook / accelerator / sprint.
- [ ] Name states the outcome, not the format.

### Pricing sanity checks

- [ ] Front end $15-$50 and stable.
- [ ] Nothing that is static info priced above ~$500.
- [ ] High-ticket has BNPL.
- [ ] Every high-ticket tier includes hand-held execution or tools, not just content.
- [ ] Add-on stack present (community + education + tools) - EP: roughly doubles revenue.
- [ ] B2B "transformational" deals structured as revshare or hybrid, not pure retainer.

## Anti-patterns

- Selling "solutions" or features instead of transformations.
- Charging premium prices for unaccompanied information.
- Raising the front-end price instead of building a back end.
- Info-only communities with no tools or peer group.
- Calling it a "course"; using the copy-paste landing page template.
- Selling in the most lucrative-looking niche you don't actually know.
- Selling a method you haven't applied (EP: essentially scamming).
- Retainer-only "we'll transform your revenue" agency pitches (EP calls these a red flag).
- Deliberately keeping an audience insecure to sustain sales (EP: don't be that).
