# Direct-response copywriting

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, attributed to him rather than presented as universal facts.

People pay to alleviate pain. Copy converts when it speaks to the deepest layer of the desire chain in the market's own words, with no jargon, and when every piece of content is structured with a conversion goal. This skill walks from research (mine real customer language) to desire mapping (surface want to underlying pain) to drafting, VSL-izing, and testing - and, for the AI era, encodes the four components (context, conditions, brand voice, bias) into a copywriting prompt.

## When to use

- User needs a landing page, sales page, VSL script, email sequence, ad, or product-promoting post.
- User asks "why isn't this converting" or "how do I make this more persuasive".
- User wants to research a customer avatar, pain points, or "what my market actually says".
- User wants a prompt that reliably produces on-brand sales copy with an LLM.
- User is about to write a promotional YouTube video, thread, or lead magnet and wants it to sell without killing retention.
- User asks how to learn copywriting.

## Core principles

**People pay to alleviate pain - write to the deepest layer of the desire chain.** EP: nobody wants to lose weight to lose weight; they want to be attractive, and beneath that, to escape the pain of not being loved or respected. Like someone with an unbearable toothache, buyers pay a lot to end pain. Separate surface wants from underlying desires and attack the emotional trigger.

**Mine real customer language before writing a word.** Run a deep-research prompt (in a deep-research mode, not normal chat) with two inputs - avatar and pain point - across Reddit, Quora, YouTube/Facebook comments, and product reviews. Extract hopes, failures, prejudices, who they blame, current solutions and what they distrust about them, forgotten past solutions, and beliefs about outside forces making it worse. Output: real quotes, recurring narratives, and a summary of the market's worldview. (EP credits Alen Sultanic via @buyerofmedia.)

**No jargon for cold traffic.** Explain in ELI5 terms - abstract words like "cognition" confuse cold prospects and kill conversions. Save insider terminology for landers aimed at audiences you've already warmed with educational content.

**Treat every piece of content like a VSL.** EP: if every YouTuber structured videos with persuasion and a conversion goal, there would be no broke YouTubers. Done wrong it hurts retention, but a balance exists in any transformational piece - and each viewer of conversion-optimized content is worth roughly 100x more, which makes up for the drop-off.

**Learn copywriting by doing, not from books.** Consuming courses makes you worse: pre-made frameworks override intuition. The best copywriters EP knows learned purely through trial and error - write, publish, watch what works, let judgment develop.

**Build an extensive swipe file.** Earning potential tracks with how many proven examples - ads, hooks, funnels, formats - you can draw on. Systematically collect what works in your market.

**Marketing skill = applied psychology plus clear writing.** Study how people perceive value, how brains structure ideas, and how to write so it is easy to digest - not tools or buzzwords.

**AI-era copywriting is four components in a prompt.** Context (the topic), conditions (rules the LLM must follow), brand voice (the style), and bias (a lens that skews output toward your favored direction). Encode domain expertise and explicit best practices into the prompt rather than letting the model infer what "good" means. EP: only someone who is already a good copywriter can write a good copywriting prompt.

**Ugly beats polished.** Unpolished creatives routinely convert better than beautiful ones - 99% of buyers don't care or notice whether something is AI-generated. Only value and clarity matter.

## Workflow

1. **Define the two inputs.** Ask the user for (a) the avatar - who exactly buys, in one sentence - and (b) the pain point in the customer's words, not the product's. If they only have a product description, work backwards: who has the problem this solves and what does that problem feel like?

2. **Research: run the customer-language prompt.** Fill in the template below and run it in a deep-research mode (not normal chat). If no deep-research tool is available, do the research manually across Reddit, Quora, YouTube/Facebook comments, and product reviews and fill the same deliverable structure. Do not skip this to "save time" - the copy will be written in your language instead of the market's.

3. **Map the desire chain.** From the research, write three layers:
   - Surface want (what they say they want, e.g. "lose weight")
   - Underlying desire (e.g. "be attractive")
   - Emotional trigger / pain (e.g. "not being loved or respected")
   Pick the deepest layer you can credibly speak to. The copy attacks that trigger; the product is the pain relief.

4. **Set traffic temperature.** Decision point: if the reader is cold (ads, first-touch content, mass-appeal post), strip all jargon and write ELI5. If the reader is warm (already consumed your educational content, on your list), insider terminology is allowed and can increase perceived fit.

5. **Draft.** Open with the pain in the market's own quoted language. Name the distrusted solutions they've tried and why those failed (from research). Present the product as the specific relief. Use the recurring narratives and worldview from the research as the story spine. Keep it clear and unpolished over beautiful.

6. **VSL-ize the content.** Whether it's a video, thread, email, or lead magnet, give it a conversion structure: problem -> why existing solutions fail -> the transformation -> the product as the mechanism -> a clear next step. Check the balance: if the persuasion is hurting retention/readability, dial back until it's a transformational piece that still has a conversion goal. Remember EP's math: a conversion-optimized viewer is worth ~100x, so some drop-off is acceptable.

7. **If using AI to draft: build the four-component prompt.** Fill the template below. Put your research and desire map in context, your rules in conditions, your voice profile in brand voice (see `brand-voice-and-authentic-ai-writing`), and the angle you want in bias. Then rewrite the output in your own hand.

8. **Test and swipe.** Publish, watch what converts, keep what works. Add every winning hook, angle, format, and funnel to the swipe file. Do not go read another copywriting course between iterations - judgment develops by doing.

## Checklists / templates

### Customer-language deep-research prompt (fill-in template)

Run in a deep-research mode. Based on the structure EP credits to Alen Sultanic via @buyerofmedia (posted April 2025).

```
AVATAR: [who exactly - e.g. "men 30-45 who started a side business and are stuck under $2k/month"]
PAIN POINT: [in their words - e.g. "I post every day and nobody buys"]

Research this avatar and pain point across Reddit, Quora, YouTube comments,
Facebook comments/groups, and product reviews (Amazon, app stores, Trustpilot,
course reviews). Use only real posts and comments; quote them verbatim.

Extract:
1. Demographic and psychographic insights
   - Hopes: what they want their life to look like once this is solved
   - Failures: what they have tried and how it went
   - Prejudices: strong opinions and biases they hold about the space
   - Who they blame for the problem (themselves, gurus, platforms, the economy...)
2. Current solutions they are using or considering
   - What they distrust or resent about each one, in their words
3. Forgotten / past solutions they used to believe in and why they abandoned them
4. Beliefs about outside forces making the problem worse (algorithms, AI,
   competition, regulation, the market, etc.)

Deliverables:
A. 30+ real quotes, grouped by the categories above, with source
B. The recurring narratives - the 5-10 stories this market tells itself
C. A one-page summary of the market's worldview, written so it can be used
   as the spine of story-driven copy
```

### Desire-chain map

| Layer | Question | Example (EP's weight-loss chain) |
|---|---|---|
| Surface want | What do they say they want? | lose weight |
| Underlying desire | What would having it give them? | be attractive |
| Emotional trigger | What pain does that escape? | not being loved or respected |

Write the copy to the bottom row. The product is the toothache relief.

### Four-component AI copywriting prompt (EP, June 2025)

```
## CONTEXT
[Topic, product, offer, price, audience. Paste the desire-chain map and the
research summary/quotes here. State traffic temperature: cold or warm.]

## CONDITIONS
[Rules the model must follow. e.g.:
- No jargon; ELI5 for cold traffic
- Open with the pain in the customer's quoted language
- Structure: problem -> failed solutions -> transformation -> product as
  mechanism -> single CTA
- Sentence length under N words; one idea per line
- Never claim results not in the context]

## BRAND VOICE
[Paste the brand-voice profile. Lowercase or not, sentence rhythm, signature
phrases, what the voice never says.]

## BIAS
[The lens that skews output your way. e.g. "Frame every alternative as
slower/more expensive than doing it yourself with this product." or
"Assume the reader is smart but burned by gurus; be blunt, never hype."]
```

### VSL-structure check for any content piece

- [ ] Opens on a pain the market actually voices (quoted or paraphrased from research)
- [ ] Names the solutions they've tried and why those failed
- [ ] Shows the transformation before explaining the mechanism
- [ ] Positions the product as the mechanism, not an afterthought
- [ ] One clear next step
- [ ] Retention/readability still holds - a viewer who never buys would still find it valuable

### Swipe file entry format

```
- Date | Market | Format (hook / ad / email / lander / funnel)
- The piece (link or verbatim)
- Why it worked (which desire-chain layer, which narrative it used)
- Metric if known
```

## Anti-patterns

- Writing to the surface want ("lose weight", "make money") instead of the pain beneath it.
- Skipping research and writing in your own vocabulary instead of the market's.
- Jargon on cold traffic. "Cognition"-type abstractions kill conversions.
- Content with no conversion goal because you're afraid of hurting retention - EP's point is the balance exists and the conversion-optimized viewer is worth ~100x.
- Consuming copywriting courses before/instead of publishing. Frameworks override intuition.
- Polishing the creative. Ugly and clear beats beautiful and vague.
- Letting an LLM infer what "good copy" means instead of encoding your best practices in the conditions.
- Letting AI fully write audience-facing copy with no rewrite pass; heavy AI users spot the tells and it destroys credibility.
- Inventing results, numbers, or testimonials not in the context.
