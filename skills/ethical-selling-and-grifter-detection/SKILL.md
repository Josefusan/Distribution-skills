# Ethical Selling & Grifter Detection

Distilled from EP's (@eptwts) knowledge base, eptwts.com. These are EP's opinionated operator heuristics, not universal facts.

This skill scores an info offer for legitimacy and helps a seller market and vet honestly. Core thesis: teach a verifiable skill and promise only tangible truths ("this works as of this date"), never guaranteed income; judge a seller by how they spend their time, not by screenshots; qualify buyers rather than selling growth info to the masses; price reasonably and refund the unhappy - the audience is the real asset and long-term trust outearns short-term extraction.

## When to use

- The user is considering buying a course, mentorship, community, or "accelerator."
- The user is writing marketing copy for an info product and wants to include income figures.
- The user is deciding whether to sell a low-ticket growth/money product to a mass audience.
- The user is building an application or vetting process for a high-ticket offer, partnership, or operator program.
- The user is setting refund policy or pricing for information.
- The user wants to understand (or is tempted by) the insecurity-driven consumer app formula.
- The user shares or plans to use profit screenshots as social proof.

## Core principles

**Never market an info product with a promised income figure.** EP: no such promise has ever had a 100% success rate, so it cannot be made without lying. Buyer outcomes are dominated by whether the person can execute, not by content quality - "make X per day" is effectively false advertising. Judge offers promising specific daily income as scams by default. Honest claims convert less initially but build a self-sustaining community with longevity.

**The line between legitimate info and grift: verifiable skill vs guaranteed results.** Teach a skill and promise only tangible truths ("this method still works as of this date"). A course teaching motion graphics is the same category as schools and non-fiction books; a guaranteed-income course is fraud.

**Judge an info seller by how they spend their time.** A legitimate teacher still spends most of their time running the business they teach. Grifter signals: profit screenshots everywhere (more screenshots = stronger signal), teaching as the main occupation, huge followings (bigger brand → more info income → staler taught model). One-off launch days get dressed up as daily income - a $35k day-one launch followed by $8k becomes "$35k/day." Legit sellers usually have smaller followings and modest flexing.

**Don't sell low-ticket "growth" info to a mass audience - qualify and screen.** Most buyers can't succeed, which invites fraud accusations. Sell mentorship only to people who can actually win; screening is worth it for four-figure-plus offers. EP's mentees all succeeded partly because he screened out everyone needing too much handholding - turning most applicants away protects the success rate and the reputation built on it.

**Price reasonably, refund anyone unhappy, make the information genuinely valuable.** Long-term trust outearns short-term extraction; the audience is the real asset. Counterpoint EP names explicitly: self-improvement creators deliberately keep audiences insecure because their business depends on it - don't be that.

**Understand the cynical insecurity-app formula; don't run it.** Find a common insecurity, build an app that "fixes" it, market to impressionable TikTok audiences - the info-product psychology applied to software subscriptions. Knowing the mechanism helps you recognize it in offers and in your own temptations.

**Vetting is a form: filter for real knowledge and achievements first.** EP's partner-program application (partners.eptwts.com) asks what the applicant is genuinely great at and what they've achieved before anything about content - the anti-larper filter - then segments by income source (operators over employees), routes faceless vs on-camera, and requires a minimum output commitment.

## Workflow

Determine the mode: **Buyer** (evaluating an offer) or **Seller** (marketing or vetting).

### Buyer mode
1. **Check the promise.** Any specific income figure or guaranteed result → score as scam by default. Tangible, dated claims ("works as of [date]", "teaches [verifiable skill]") → continue.
2. **Check how the seller spends their time.** Is teaching their main occupation? Do they still run the business they teach? Look for evidence of the underlying business, not screenshots of its proceeds.
3. **Count the screenshots.** Profit screenshots everywhere is a grifter signal, not proof. Look for proof-plus-process (client transformations, exact steps) instead.
4. **Check the following size and flex level.** Huge following + heavy flexing → stale model, higher grift probability. Smaller following + modest flexing → more likely legitimate.
5. **Decode "per day" claims.** Ask whether a launch-day peak is being presented as a daily rate.
6. **Check price vs format.** Static information above ~$500 with no hand-held execution is exploitative (EP's threshold). Is there a refund policy?
7. **Check whether they screen.** A four-figure mentorship that accepts everyone is optimizing for extraction; one that turns most applicants away is protecting outcomes.
8. **Run the scorecard** and give a verdict with the strongest signals.

### Seller mode
1. **Strip income promises from all copy.** Replace with the verifiable skill taught and a dated truth statement. Keep transformation framing (see `info-products-sell-transformation`) but never a guaranteed number.
2. **Audit your own proof.** Replace profit screenshots with process and client transformations. If your only proof is your own earnings, build client results first.
3. **Decide who you will refuse to sell to.** If the offer is growth/money info: do not sell it low-ticket to a mass audience. For four-figure-plus offers, add an application (Tally) using the vetting template below and turn away applicants who need more handholding than you can deliver.
4. **Set price and refund policy.** Static info stays under ~$500; refund anyone unhappy without a fight.
5. **Check the dependency test.** Does your business depend on buyers staying insecure or failing? If yes, redesign toward a model where their success is your marketing.
6. **Run the scorecard on your own offer** before launch and fix any red flags.

## Checklists / templates

### Legitimacy scorecard (score each 0-2; higher = more legitimate)

| # | Signal | 0 (red) | 1 | 2 (green) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Promise | specific income figure / guaranteed result | vague outcome hype | verifiable skill + dated tangible truth |
| 2 | Seller's time | teaching is the main occupation | mixed | still runs the business they teach |
| 3 | Proof type | profit screenshots everywhere | own results only | client transformations + exact process |
| 4 | Following & flex | huge following, heavy flexing | moderate | smaller following, modest flexing |
| 5 | "Per day" math | launch peak presented as daily | unclear | honest ranges or none |
| 6 | Price vs format | static info > ~$500, no execution support | borderline | low-ticket info or high-ticket with hand-held execution |
| 7 | Qualification | sells to anyone | soft filter | screens applicants; turns most away |
| 8 | Refunds | none / hostile | conditional | refunds anyone unhappy |
| 9 | Audience dependency | business needs buyers insecure/failing | neutral | buyers' success is the marketing |

Verdict: 0-6 scam by default; 7-12 proceed with caution and verify; 13-18 likely legitimate.

### Honest claim rewrite

```
Before: "Make $500/day with this system."
After:  "Teaches [skill]. This method works as of [date]. Results depend on your execution; here's what it took for [named client/example]."
```

### Applicant-vetting template (adapted from EP's partners form)

Use as a Tally application in front of any four-figure-plus mentorship, partnership, or operator program. Read every application; reject most.

**Section 1 - Anti-larper filter (weighted highest)**
1. What do you do?
2. What's your specialized knowledge? What are you genuinely great at?
3. What have you achieved with what you do? What are you most proud of?
4. Links to your work (X, YouTube, portfolio, GitHub - anything that shows what you do).

**Section 2 - Situation segmentation**
5. How much does it currently net you? (Under $2k / $2k-$5k / $5k-$10k / $10k-$25k / $25k-$50k / $50k-$100k / $100k+ per month / Prefer not to say)
6. Is that income from a job or your own business? (Job / Own business or freelancing / Mix)
7. Do you have any products or services you sell? If yes, what?

**Section 3 - Fit and routing**
8. Are you comfortable on camera? Can you show your face? Prefer anonymous? Can you record with your voice? (routes faceless vs on-camera)
9. How would you rate your writing skills? (Strong / Decent / Weak)
10. Experience with social media; have you made content before, and how did it go?

**Section 4 - Commitment (minimum output)**
11. How much free time can you commit per week? (Under 5 / 5-10 / 10-20 / 20+ hours)
12. Can you [program-specific minimum, e.g., write 2-3 posts per day and record one video per week]? (Yes / No / Not sure)

**Section 5 - Goal alignment**
13. What do you want this to do for you? What's the goal outcome?
14. What opportunities should it bring? What do you want to sell?
15. Contact: email + primary comms handle (EP requires Telegram).

**Rejection criteria (from EP's model):** no demonstrable specialized knowledge or achievements; needs more handholding than you can deliver; cannot meet the minimum output commitment; goal doesn't feed a sellable offer.

## Anti-patterns

- Income-figure marketing ("$X/day", "guaranteed").
- Screenshot walls as proof.
- Dressing a launch-day peak as recurring daily income.
- Selling low-ticket growth/money info to everyone.
- Accepting every applicant into a high-ticket program.
- Charging premium prices for unaccompanied information; refusing refunds.
- Keeping the audience insecure to sustain sales.
- Running the insecurity-app formula on impressionable audiences (EP: understand it, don't run it).
- Selling methods you haven't applied yourself.
